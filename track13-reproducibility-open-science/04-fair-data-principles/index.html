    🎯 Conceptual Overview & Derivation

    The Operationalization of FAIR: Beyond Aspirational Manifestos

    In 2016, a consortium of international researchers and data stewards formulated the FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management: Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability (Wilkinson et al., 2016). While widely celebrated, early adoptions frequently treated FAIR as a subjective checklist or a qualitative aspiration. In modern dataset engineering, FAIR must be operationalized as an executable, multi-criteria mathematical specification (Jacobsen et al., 2020; Mons et al., 2017).

    A central insight of rigorous data stewardship is that FAIR applies equally to data payload (\(\mathcal{D}\)) and metadata descriptions (\(\mathcal{M}\)), but their lifecycles are decoupled. Furthermore, FAIR is emphatically not synonymous with Open Data: a highly confidential clinical dataset containing protected health information can achieve 100% FAIR compliance if its access restrictions are governed by automated, standardized, and machine-actionable authorization protocols (Jacobsen et al., 2020).

    +---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        THE CANONICAL 15 ATOMIC FACETS OF FAIR DATA                                |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| FINDABLE (F)             | ACCESSIBLE (A)          | INTEROPERABLE (I)       | REUSABLE (R)               |
| - F1: Global Unique PID  | - A1: Open Std Protocol | - I1: Formal Knowledge  | - R1: Plural Attributes    |
| - F2: Rich Metadata      |   * A1.1: Free/Standard | - I2: FAIR Vocabularies | - R1.1: Clear SPDX License |
| - F3: Explicit PID Link  |   * A1.2: Auth Support  | - I3: Qualified Links   | - R1.2: Detailed Provenance|
| - F4: Searchable Index   | - A2: Tombstone Record  |                         | - R1.3: Community Standard |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

    Mathematical Formulations of FAIR Data Principles

    To evaluate and audit compliance systematically, we formulate four formal mathematical definitions governing metric tensors, searchability distributions, protocol longevity invariants, and reusability decay rates.

    Definition 13.4.1: The 15 FAIR Facets & Multi-Criteria Maturity Metric Tensor

    Let a digital research object \(\mathcal{R} = (\mathcal{D}, \mathcal{M})\) consist of a dataset payload \(\mathcal{D}\) and its associated metadata manifest \(\mathcal{M}\). The compliance of \(\mathcal{R}\) is evaluated against the 15 atomic sub-principles defined by Wilkinson et al. (2016), partitioned across four cardinal pillars \(\mathcal{P} = \{\mathbf{F}, \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{I}, \mathbf{R}\}\):

    \[\boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathcal{R}) = \left( \mu_{F_1}, \dots, \mu_{F_4}, \mu_{A_{1.1}}, \mu_{A_{1.2}}, \mu_{A_2}, \mu_{I_1}, \mu_{I_2}, \mu_{I_3}, \mu_{R_1}, \mu_{R_{1.1}}, \mu_{R_{1.2}}, \mu_{R_{1.3}} \right)^\top \in [0, 1]^{15}\]

    where each coordinate \(\mu_i(\mathcal{R}) \in [0, 1]\) represents the audited compliance score of facet \(i\).

    For each cardinal pillar \(p \in \mathcal{P}\), the intra-pillar mean compliance \(\bar{\mu}_p\) is:

    \[\bar{\mu}_p = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_p|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{F}_p} \mu_i(\mathcal{R})\]

    While a linear weighted combination yields a baseline maturity index:

    \[\Phi_{\text{linear}}(\mathcal{R}) = \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} w_p \bar{\mu}_p, \quad \text{where } \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} w_p = 1, \; w_p > 0\]

    linear aggregation violates the foundational Non-Compensatory Axiom of Data Stewardship: high semantic interoperability cannot compensate for complete inaccessibility. If a dataset is inaccessible (\(\bar{\mu}_A = 0\)), its real-world scientific utility is zero.

    To enforce non-compensability, we formulate the Strict FAIR Maturity Metric \(\Phi_{\text{strict}}(\mathcal{R})\) using a generalized Hölder / Cobb-Douglas harmonic aggregation with penalty exponent \(\rho > 0\):

    \[\Phi_{\text{strict}}(\mathcal{R}) = \left( \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \beta_p \cdot (\bar{\mu}_p + \epsilon)^{-\rho} \right)^{-1/\rho}\]

    where \(\sum_{p} \beta_p = 1\), \(\beta_p > 0\), and \(\epsilon > 0\) is a stability regularization constant. As the penalty exponent \(\rho \to \infty\), the aggregation converges to the strict Leontief bottleneck:

    \[\lim_{\rho \to \infty} \Phi_{\text{strict}}(\mathcal{R}) = \min_{p \in \{\mathbf{F}, \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{I}, \mathbf{R}\}} \bar{\mu}_p\]

    guaranteeing that any systemic failure in an individual pillar decisively gates the overall certification of the dataset (Jacobsen et al., 2020; Wang & Strong, 1996).

    Definition 13.4.2: Findability Searchability Index & Semantic Triplet Recall

    Findability dictates that digital resources can be discovered by both human investigators and autonomous computational agents. Let \(\mathcal{Q}\) represent the distribution of natural language and SPARQL semantic search queries issued over a research domain.

    Let \(\mathbf{e}(q) \in \mathbb{R}^d\) and \(\mathbf{e}(\mathcal{M}) \in \mathbb{R}^d\) represent the normalized semantic embeddings of query \(q\) and metadata manifest \(\mathcal{M}\) in a shared representation space. We define the Semantic Findability Retrieval Score \(\mathcal{S}_{\text{find}}(\mathcal{R}, q)\):

    \[\mathcal{S}_{\text{find}}(\mathcal{R}, q) = \sigma\left( \langle \mathbf{e}(q), \mathbf{e}(\mathcal{M}) \rangle + \gamma_{\text{PID}} \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{PID}_{\text{valid}}(\mathcal{R})) + \gamma_{\text{idx}} \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{Indexed}(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{I}_{\text{cat}})) \right)\]

    where \(\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}\) is the sigmoid activation, \(\gamma_{\text{PID}} > 0\) is the persistent identifier incentive bonus, \(\gamma_{\text{idx}} > 0\) is the indexed repository bonus, and \(\mathbb{I}(\cdot)\) is the indicator function.

    Theorem 13.4.1 (Findability Lower Bound):

    If a dataset lacks an indexed catalog registration (\(\mathbb{I}(\text{Indexed}) = 0\)) and lacks a resolvable PID (\(\mathbb{I}(\text{PID}) = 0\)), then under standard cosine embeddings \(\langle \mathbf{e}(q), \mathbf{e}(\mathcal{M}) \rangle \le 1.0\):

    \[\mathcal{S}_{\text{find}}(\mathcal{R}, q) \le \sigma(1.0) \approx 0.731\]

    Whereas an indexed, PID-endowed manifest with identical textual content achieves:

    \[\mathcal{S}_{\text{find}}(\mathcal{R}, q) \ge \sigma(1.0 + \gamma_{\text{PID}} + \gamma_{\text{idx}}) \ge 0.952 \quad (\text{for } \gamma_{\text{PID}} = 1.0, \gamma_{\text{idx}} = 1.0)\]

    This explains why unindexed local files become functionally invisible to automated discovery crawlers (Brickley et al., 2019; Wilkinson et al., 2016).

    Definition 13.4.3: Accessibility Protocol Invariants & The A2 Tombstone Invariant

    Accessibility mandates that data and metadata are retrievable using their identifier via open, standardized communications protocols (\(A_1\)), allowing for authentication and authorization where necessary (\(A_{1.2}\)).

    Crucially, Principle \(A_2\) dictates that:

    \[\text{Metadata are accessible, even when the data are no longer available.}\]

    Let \(T_{\text{data}}\) be the active physical hosting lifespan of dataset payload \(\mathcal{D}\), and let \(T_{\text{meta}}\) be the archival lifespan of metadata manifest \(\mathcal{M}\). The FAIR Accessibility Longevity Invariant requires:

    \[T_{\text{meta}} \gg T_{\text{data}}, \quad \text{with } \lim_{t \to \infty} P\left( \text{Resolvable}(\mathcal{M}_t) \right) = 1\]

    When a dataset payload \(\mathcal{D}\) is decommissioned—due to cloud storage costs, physical media deprecation, privacy redactions, or GDPR "Right to be Forgotten" mandates—the server must never return an HTTP 404 Not Found. Instead, the endpoint must return an HTTP 410 Gone containing a cryptographically signed Tombstone Manifest \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{tombstone}}\):

    \[\mathcal{M}_{\text{tombstone}} = \left( \text{DOI}, t_{\text{retract}}, \text{Reason}, \mathcal{H}_{\text{SHA256}}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{retired}}), \text{ContactURI} \right)\]

    This ensures that downstream literature citing the DOI can verify historical provenance and validate cryptographic digests without silent link breakage (Jacobsen et al., 2020; Stodden et al., 2016).

    Definition 13.4.4: Semantic Interoperability Ontology Alignment & Reusability Decay

    Interoperability requires knowledge representations using formal, accessible, and broadly applicable ontologies (\(I_1\)), vocabularies that themselves follow FAIR (\(I_2\)), and qualified cross-references (\(I_3\)). Reusability requires plural relevant attributes (\(R_1\)), clear SPDX licenses (\(R_{1.1}\)), detailed provenance (\(R_{1.2}\)), and domain standards (\(R_{1.3}\)).

    Following the landmark empirical study by Vines et al. (2014) and quality economics by Wang & Strong (1996), uncurated datasets suffer exponential reusability decay over time:

    \[\mathcal{R}_{\text{decay}}(t) = \mathcal{R}_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda t}\]

    where \(\mathcal{R}_0 = 1.0\) is initial reusability and \(\lambda > 0\) is the decay constant. In uncurated, non-FAIR datasets, Vines et al. (2014) measured an annual decay rate of \(\lambda_0 \approx 0.170 \text{ year}^{-1}\), yielding a half-life of:

    \[t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln(2)}{\lambda_0} \approx \frac{0.6931}{0.170} \approx 4.08 \text{ years}\]

    Under comprehensive FAIR stewardship (\(\Phi_{\text{strict}} \ge 0.90\)), persistent PIDs prevent URL rot, standardized ontologies eliminate semantic drift, and machine-readable licenses remove legal ambiguity. The effective decay constant scales inversely with the strict FAIR index:

    \[\lambda_{\text{FAIR}} = \lambda_0 \cdot \left( 1 - \Phi_{\text{strict}}(\mathcal{R}) \right) + \lambda_{\text{floor}}\]

    where \(\lambda_{\text{floor}} \approx 0.015 \text{ year}^{-1}\) represents unavoidable semantic drift in domain ontologies. For a FAIR-certified dataset with \(\Phi_{\text{strict}} = 0.95\):

    \[\lambda_{\text{FAIR}} = 0.170 \times (1 - 0.95) + 0.015 = 0.0085 + 0.015 = 0.0235 \text{ year}^{-1}\]

    yielding an extended functional half-life of:

    \[t_{1/2}^{\text{FAIR}} = \frac{\ln(2)}{0.0235} \approx 29.5 \text{ years}\]

    representing a 7.2-fold increase in scientific and commercial durability (Vines et al., 2014; Wilkinson et al., 2016).

    💻 Runnable Executable Python Implementation

    The following production-grade implementation, FAIRComplianceAssessmentEngine, provides complete mathematical validation and auditing for the FAIR Guiding Principles. It implements: 1. Pydantic V2 models for all 15 atomic FAIR facets, digital resources, and HTTP 410 tombstone records. 2. Exact calculation of intra-pillar compliance \(\bar{\mu}_p\), linear index \(\Phi_{\text{linear}}\), and non-compensatory harmonic index \(\Phi_{\text{strict}}\). 3. Automated generation and verification of RFC-compliant HTTP 410 Tombstone manifests for retired payloads. 4. Vines exponential reusability decay simulations over multi-decade horizons.

    """
FAIRComplianceAssessmentEngine: Production-grade validation and mathematical auditing
for the 15 atomic FAIR Guiding Principles, non-compensatory harmonic scoring,
and RFC-compliant HTTP 410 Tombstone longevity invariants.
"""

from __future__ import annotations
import math
import hashlib
from typing import Dict, List, Optional, Set, Tuple, Any
from enum import Enum
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator, model_validator


class FAIRPillar(str, Enum):
    FINDABLE = "Findable"
    ACCESSIBLE = "Accessible"
    INTEROPERABLE = "Interoperable"
    REUSABLE = "Reusable"


class FAIRFacetID(str, Enum):
    F1 = "F1_Persistent_Identifier"
    F2 = "F2_Rich_Metadata"
    F3 = "F3_Metadata_Includes_PID"
    F4 = "F4_Searchable_Registry"
    A1_1 = "A1_1_Open_Protocol"
    A1_2 = "A1_2_Auth_Protocol"
    A2 = "A2_Metadata_Longevity"
    I1 = "I1_Knowledge_Representation"
    I2 = "I2_FAIR_Vocabularies"
    I3 = "I3_Qualified_References"
    R1 = "R1_Plural_Attributes"
    R1_1 = "R1_1_Clear_SPDX_License"
    R1_2 = "R1_2_Detailed_Provenance"
    R1_3 = "R1_3_Community_Standards"


class FAIRFacetScore(BaseModel):
    facet: FAIRFacetID
    pillar: FAIRPillar
    compliance: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.0, description="Compliance score in [0.0, 1.0]")
    weight: float = Field(default=1.0, gt=0.0, description="Intra-pillar importance weight")
    evidence: str = Field(..., min_length=1)


class DigitalResourceManifest(BaseModel):
    name: str = Field(..., min_length=1)
    doi: Optional[str] = None
    access_url: Optional[str] = None
    license_spdx: Optional[str] = None
    ontology_uris: List[str] = Field(default_factory=list)
    has_tombstone_policy: bool = False
    is_indexed_in_search: bool = False
    is_payload_active: bool = True
    payload_sha256: Optional[str] = None


class TombstoneRecord(BaseModel):
    doi: str
    retirement_timestamp: str
    retirement_reason: str
    original_payload_sha256: str
    metadata_archive_url: str
    custodian_contact: str
    http_status_code: int = 410

    @field_validator("http_status_code")
    @classmethod
    def verify_http_410(cls, v: int) -> int:
        if v != 410:
            raise ValueError(f"Tombstone must return HTTP 410 Gone under FAIR A2, got {v}")
        return v


class FAIRAuditReport(BaseModel):
    resource_name: str
    pillar_means: Dict[FAIRPillar, float]
    phi_linear: float
    phi_strict: float
    is_certified: bool
    reusability_half_life_years: float
    diagnostics: List[str]


class FAIRComplianceAssessmentEngine:
    """
    Mathematical engine implementing Definitions 13.4.1 through 13.4.4.
    """

    def __init__(self, rho_penalty: float = 2.0, epsilon: float = 1e-6):
        self.rho = rho_penalty
        self.epsilon = epsilon
        self.pillar_weights: Dict[FAIRPillar, float] = {
            FAIRPillar.FINDABLE: 0.25,
            FAIRPillar.ACCESSIBLE: 0.25,
            FAIRPillar.INTEROPERABLE: 0.25,
            FAIRPillar.REUSABLE: 0.25,
        }

    def evaluate_resource(
        self, resource: DigitalResourceManifest

        scores.append(

                facet=FAIRFacetID.F2,
                pillar=FAIRPillar.FINDABLE,
                compliance=0.95 if has_rich_meta else 0.3,

            )
        )

        scores.append(
            FAIRFacetScore(
                facet=FAIRFacetID.F3,
                pillar=FAIRPillar.FINDABLE,
                compliance=1.0 if has_doi else 0.2,
                evidence="Metadata record explicitly cites primary PID",
            )
        )

        scores.append(
            FAIRFacetScore(
                facet=FAIRFacetID.F4,
                pillar=FAIRPillar.FINDABLE,

                evidence="Indexed in Google Dataset Search / Zenodo / Dataverse" if resource.is_indexed_in_search else "Unindexed dark dataset",

        )

        # --- ACCESSIBLE (A1.1, A1.2, A2) ---

        scores.append(

                facet=FAIRFacetID.A1_1,
                pillar=FAIRPillar.ACCESSIBLE,

                pillar=FAIRPillar.ACCESSIBLE,

        scores.append(
            FAIRFacetScore(
                facet=FAIRFacetID.R1,

                facet=FAIRFacetID.R1_2,

                compliance=0.88,

                facet=FAIRFacetID.R1_3,
                pillar=FAIRPillar.REUSABLE,
                compliance=0.92,

    def compute_maturity_metrics(
        self, scores: List[FAIRFacetScore]
    ) -> Tuple[Dict[FAIRPillar, float], float, float]:
        """
        Computes intra-pillar means, linear maturity Phi_linear,
        and non-compensatory harmonic maturity Phi_strict.
        """
        pillar_groups: Dict[FAIRPillar, List[float]] = {p: [] for p in FAIRPillar}
        for s in scores:
            pillar_groups[s.pillar].append(s.compliance)

        pillar_means: Dict[FAIRPillar, float] = {}
        for p, vals in pillar_groups.items():
            pillar_means[p] = sum(vals) / len(vals) if vals else 0.0

        # Linear aggregation
        phi_linear = sum(self.pillar_weights[p] * pillar_means[p] for p in FAIRPillar)

        # Non-compensatory harmonic aggregation: ( sum beta_p * (mu_p + eps)^(-rho) )^(-1/rho)
        harmonic_sum = sum(
            self.pillar_weights[p] * ((pillar_means[p] + self.epsilon) ** (-self.rho))
            for p in FAIRPillar
        )
        phi_strict = (harmonic_sum) ** (-1.0 / self.rho)

        return pillar_means, phi_linear, phi_strict

    def simulate_reusability_half_life(self, phi_strict: float) -> float:
        """
        Calculates functional reusability half-life (years) under Vines decay formulation:
        lambda = lambda_0 * (1 - Phi_strict) + lambda_floor
        """
        lambda_0 = 0.170  # Annual uncurated decay (Vines et al., 2014)
        lambda_floor = 0.015  # Unavoidable domain ontology evolution
        effective_lambda = lambda_0 * (1.0 - min(1.0, phi_strict)) + lambda_floor
        half_life_years = math.log(2.0) / effective_lambda
        return half_life_years

    def generate_tombstone(
        self, resource: DigitalResourceManifest, reason: str, custodian: str
    ) -> TombstoneRecord:
        """
        Generates an RFC-compliant HTTP 410 Tombstone record under FAIR A2.
        """
        if not resource.doi:
            raise ValueError("Cannot generate tombstone for a resource lacking a permanent DOI.")

        payload_hash = resource.payload_sha256 or hashlib.sha256(b"dummy_payload").hexdigest()
        return TombstoneRecord(
            doi=resource.doi,
            retirement_timestamp="2026-11-13T08:00:00Z",
            retirement_reason=reason,
            original_payload_sha256=payload_hash,
            metadata_archive_url=f"https://zenodo.org/records/tombstone/{resource.doi.replace('/', '_')}",
            custodian_contact=custodian,
            http_status_code=410,
        )

    def run_full_audit(
        self, resource: DigitalResourceManifest, min_strict_threshold: float = 0.80
    ) -> FAIRAuditReport:
        """
        Executes end-to-end evaluation, checking certification criteria and diagnosing deficits.
        """
        scores = self.evaluate_resource(resource)
        pillar_means, phi_linear, phi_strict = self.compute_maturity_metrics(scores)
        half_life = self.simulate_reusability_half_life(phi_strict)

        diagnostics: List[str] = []
        for p, mean in pillar_means.items():
            if mean < 0.70:
                diagnostics.append(f"Pillar {p.value} has critical deficit: {mean:.3f} < 0.700")

        if phi_strict < min_strict_threshold:
            diagnostics.append(f"Strict index {phi_strict:.3f} below certification gate {min_strict_threshold:.3f}")

        is_certified = (phi_strict >= min_strict_threshold) and all(m >= 0.70 for m in pillar_means.values())

        return FAIRAuditReport(
            resource_name=resource.name,
            pillar_means=pillar_means,
            phi_linear=phi_linear,
            phi_strict=phi_strict,
            is_certified=is_certified,
            reusability_half_life_years=half_life,
            diagnostics=diagnostics,
        )

    Exercise 1: Calculating Strict vs. Linear FAIR Maturity under Single-Pillar Failure

    An autonomous data crawler audits an institutional biomedical archive containing 5,000 legacy clinical datasets. The automated audit reveals the following pillar compliance means: - \(\bar{\mu}_F = 0.96\) (High findability with DataCite DOIs) - \(\bar{\mu}_I = 0.92\) (Mapped to SNOMED-CT and Schema.org) - \(\bar{\mu}_R = 0.90\) (Clear CC-BY-4.0 licenses and PROV-O records) - \(\bar{\mu}_A = 0.05\) (The university storage SAN decommissioned the physical FTP servers, returning unhandled connection timeouts)

    1. Compute the linear maturity index \(\Phi_{\text{linear}}\) assuming equal pillar weights (\(w_p = 0.25\)).
    2. Compute the strict non-compensatory harmonic index \(\Phi_{\text{strict}}\) under penalty exponent \(\rho = 2.0\) with stability constant \(\epsilon = 10^{-6}\).
    3. Analyze the policy implications of using \(\Phi_{\text{linear}}\) versus \(\Phi_{\text{strict}}\) for enterprise repository certification.
    Reveal Complete Step-by-Step Solution

    Step 1: Calculating Linear FAIR Maturity \(\Phi_{\text{linear}}\)

    Under equal weights \(w_p = 0.25\):

    \[\Phi_{\text{linear}} = \sum_{p \in \{\mathbf{F}, \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{I}, \mathbf{R}\}} w_p \bar{\mu}_p = 0.25 \times (0.96 + 0.05 + 0.92 + 0.90)\]
    \[\Phi_{\text{linear}} = 0.25 \times 2.83 = 0.7075 \quad (70.8\%)\]

    Step 2: Calculating Strict Non-Compensatory Maturity \(\Phi_{\text{strict}}\)

    Under Definition 13.4.1 with \(\rho = 2.0\), \(\beta_p = 0.25\), and \(\bar{\mu}_A = 0.05\):

    \[\Phi_{\text{strict}} = \left( \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \beta_p \cdot (\bar{\mu}_p + \epsilon)^{-\rho} \right)^{-1/\rho} = \left( 0.25 \left[ (0.96)^{-2} + (0.05)^{-2} + (0.92)^{-2} + (0.90)^{-2} \right] \right)^{-1/2}\]

    Computing each term: - \((0.96)^{-2} \approx 1.085\) - \((0.05)^{-2} = \frac{1}{0.0025} = 400.000\) - \((0.92)^{-2} \approx 1.181\) - \((0.90)^{-2} \approx 1.235\)

    Summing the terms:

    \[\sum = 1.085 + 400.000 + 1.181 + 1.235 = 403.501\]

    Multiplying by weight \(\beta = 0.25\):

    \[\text{Weighted Sum} = 0.25 \times 403.501 = 100.875\]

    Taking the inverse exponent \(-1/2\):

    \[\Phi_{\text{strict}} = (100.875)^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{100.875}} \approx \frac{1}{10.0437} \approx 0.0996 \quad (9.96\%)\]

    Step 3: Policy Implications Analysis

    • Linear Metric Failure: Under \(\Phi_{\text{linear}}\), the archive scores 70.8%, achieving a passing "Silver" certification tier in many naïve repository evaluation schemes. However, researchers downloading the data face complete failure because the underlying files do not exist. Linear averaging permits high scores in metadata documentation to mask a total operational catastrophe.
    • Strict Metric Integrity: Under \(\Phi_{\text{strict}}\), the score collapses to 9.96%, reflecting the real-world reality that an inaccessible dataset has virtually zero functional utility. Non-compensatory harmonic evaluation prevents deceptive reporting and guarantees that certification gates block compromised archives.

    Reveal Complete Step-by-Step Solution

    Step 1: HTTP 404 Not Found vs. HTTP 410 Gone

    • HTTP 410 Gone is an authoritative, permanent status code defined in RFC 9110. It informs client agents that the target resource was intentionally removed, that no forwarding address exists, and that the removal is permanent. Under FAIR Principle \(A_2\), returning HTTP 410 accompanied by a machine-readable tombstone preserves citation integrity in scientific literature by proving that the cited DOI was valid and historically authentic.
