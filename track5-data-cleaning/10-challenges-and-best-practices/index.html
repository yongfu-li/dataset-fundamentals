    Data cleaning carries a paradox: well-intentioned transformations frequently introduce catastrophic statistical artifacts if decoupled from causal ordering and partition governance. Among production machine learning failures, data leakage and unmonitored distributional drift are the leading causes of offline-to-online performance collapses [Kaufman2012, Sculley2015].

    Enterprise dataset engineering prevents these silent failures through Leakage Sentinel Audits, Cryptographic Provenance Semirings [Green2007], and Population Stability Index (PSI) Circuit Breakers [Schelter2018, Northcutt2021].

    2. Theoretical Formalisms: Leakage, Semirings & Drift Metrics

    1. Three Structural Vectors of Leakage [Kaufman2012]

    Type I: Causal Target Leakage (\(Y \longrightarrow X_{\text{leak}}\))

    In legitimate prediction, causal information propagates forward from predictor attributes to future target outcomes (\(X_j \longrightarrow Y\)). Target leakage arises when an engineered feature \(X_{\text{leak}}\) is recorded after or as a direct administrative consequence of \(Y\):

    \[ Y \longrightarrow X_{\text{leak}} \implies I\left( X_{\text{leak}}; Y \right) \to H(Y) \]

    Example: Training an inpatient mortality predictor using hospital_billing_autopsy_code or predicting loan default using collection_agency_fee.

    Type II: Train-Test Distributional Contamination (\(\theta(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}})\))

    Occurs when preprocessing operators (imputers, scalers, PCA rotations, SMOTE generators, or fuzzy record linkages) share state across splits, or when duplicate records span both partitions:

    \[ \hat{\theta} = g\left( \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}} \right) \implies I\left( \hat{\theta}; \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}} \right) > 0 \]

    Type III: Temporal Filtration Inversion (Look-Ahead Bias)

    In sequential event streams, predictions at forecast horizon \(t\) may only condition on events inside the measurable filtration \(\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(\{X_s : s \le t\})\). Random shuffling across time allows future covariates \(\mathbf{x}_{t'}\) (\(t' > t\)) to leak into training gradients:

    \[ \max_{t \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} t_{\text{event}} \ge \min_{t' \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}} t'_{\text{event}} \implies \text{Look-Ahead Contamination} \]

    2. Provenance Semirings & Cryptographic Lineage Chaining

    When regulations (such as GDPR Article 17 "Right to Erasure") mandate purging all derived artifacts traced back to a deleted user record, ad-hoc logs collapse. Algebraic data lineage models derivations as Provenance Semirings \((\mathbb{K}, \oplus, \otimes, 0, 1)\) [Green2007]: - Alternative Derivation (\(\oplus\)): Relational Union (\(\cup\)) or Duplicate Aggregation. - Joint Derivation (\(\otimes\)): Relational Join (\(\Join\)) or Multi-table Feature Merge.

    Operationally, every transformation stage seals its execution graph with an immutable Merkle / SHA-256 lineage chain:

    \[ H_k = \operatorname{SHA-256}\left( H_{k-1} \;\|\; \text{CodeCommitHash} \;\|\; \text{ConfigJSON} \;\|\; \text{RandomSeed} \right) \]

    3. Production Drift Sentinels: PSI & Kolmogorov-Smirnov Distance

    Because live environments shift continuously after deployment, pipelines monitor distributional divergence continuously across production batches:

    Population Stability Index (PSI)

    Measures binned discrete shift between baseline training density \(P_b\) and live production density \(Q_b\):

    \[ \text{PSI} = \sum_{b=1}^B \left( P_b - Q_b \right) \ln\left( \frac{P_b}{Q_b} \right) \]
    • \(\text{PSI} < 0.10\): Minimal shift (Green — Stable baseline).
    • \(0.10 \le \text{PSI} \le 0.25\): Moderate population drift (Yellow — Monitoring alert).
    • \(\text{PSI} > 0.25\): Severe distributional shift (Red — Trigger automated circuit breaker & model retraining!).

    Covariate Shift vs. Concept Drift

    • Covariate Shift: Input distribution changes while conditional mapping stays fixed: \(P_{\text{train}}(X) \ne P_{\text{deploy}}(X)\), \(P_{\text{train}}(Y \mid X) = P_{\text{deploy}}(Y \mid X)\).
    • Concept Drift: Fundamental world mechanism shifts over time: \(P_{\text{train}}(Y \mid X) \ne P_{\text{deploy}}(Y \mid X)\).

    Industrial Best Practice: Mutual Information Proxy Gates Before Model Fitting

    Automatically execute univariate Mutual Information (\(I(X_j; Y)\)) and point-biserial correlation sweeps on training columns prior to modeling. Any feature reporting unusually high predictability (\(I(X_j; Y) > 0.45\) or single-feature AUC \(> 0.90\) in noisy domains) must pass manual causal timestamp verification before entering production!

    Common Pitfall / Anti-Pattern: Silent Schema & Categorical Drift Mutations

    Upstream engineering teams frequently rename categorical ENUM strings ("mobile_ios" \(\to\) "iOS") or convert user_id integers to padded strings without notifying ML teams. Untyped pipelines silently assign unknown categories to NaN or fallback buckets, degrading live inference accuracy cleanly without throwing Python exceptions.

    3. Landmark Empirical Case Study: Kaggle Healthcare Mortality Leakage

    In a documented predictive healthcare benchmark modeling 30-day ICU mortality: 1. The \(0.998\) Mirage: Top leaderboard teams achieved a seemingly impossible ROC-AUC of \(0.998\) predicting patient mortality. 2. Causal Discovery: Forensic audit revealed a feature called encounter_sequence_id. Hospital clerks closed deceased patient administrative accounts immediately upon transfer to the morgue, systematically assigning distinct monotonic batch ID blocks to non-survivors. Models learned hospital administrative billing quirks rather than human physiology. 3. Production Remediation: Purging encounter_sequence_id lowered CV AUC from \(0.998\) to a clinically grounded \(0.814\)—matching out-of-hospital generalization (\(0.811\)) while preventing a fatal model deployment [Kaufman2012, Sculley2015].

    Causal Audited (Clinical Vitals Only) \(0.814\) \(0.811\) Certified Safe Deployment

    5. Production-Grade Python Verification Engine

    The following Python module uses Pydantic V2 to automate Type I Causal Proxy Audits (Mutual Information detection), Type II Split Duplicate Detection, Type III Temporal Inversion Checks, Population Stability Index (PSI) drift alerts, and SHA-256 Provenance Digests.

    """
production_data_leakage_sentinel.py
Pydantic V2 verified Sentinel Auditing Type I/II/III Leakage, PSI Drift & Cryptographic Lineage.
"""

import hashlib
import json
from typing import Dict, List
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from sklearn.feature_selection import mutual_info_classif


class LeakageSentinelReport(BaseModel):
    """Pydantic V2 schema reporting detected leakage defects, PSI drift, and lineage hash."""
    type1_proxy_features: List[str]
    type2_split_duplicates_found: int
    type3_temporal_leak_detected: bool
    psi_stable_score: float = Field(..., lt=0.10)
    psi_drifted_score: float = Field(..., gt=0.25)
    sha256_lineage_digest: str = Field(..., min_length=64, max_length=64)


class DataLeakageSentinel:
    """Automated Governance Sentinel validating causal safety, split purity, and PSI drift."""

    def __init__(self, mi_threshold: float = 0.40):
        self.mi_threshold = mi_threshold

    def audit_target_proxies(self, X: pd.DataFrame, y: pd.Series) -> Dict[str, float]:
        """Type I Check: Flags features exhibiting suspiciously extreme Mutual Information."""
        num_df = X.select_dtypes(include=[np.number]).fillna(-9999.0)
        mi_scores = mutual_info_classif(num_df, y, random_state=42)
        return {col: round(float(mi), 4) for col, mi in zip(num_df.columns, mi_scores) if mi >= self.mi_threshold}

    @staticmethod
    def audit_split_contamination(X_train: pd.DataFrame, X_test: pd.DataFrame) -> int:
        """Type II Check: Identifies identical row hashes existing in both train and test partitions."""
        train_hashes = set(pd.util.hash_pandas_object(X_train, index=False))
        test_hashes = set(pd.util.hash_pandas_object(X_test, index=False))
        return len(train_hashes.intersection(test_hashes))

    @staticmethod
    def audit_temporal_leakage(train_timestamps: pd.Series, test_timestamps: pd.Series) -> bool:
        """Type III Check: Returns True if max(train_time) >= min(test_time)."""
        return bool(pd.to_datetime(train_timestamps).max() >= pd.to_datetime(test_timestamps).min())

    @staticmethod
    def compute_psi(baseline: np.ndarray, current: np.ndarray, bins: int = 10) -> float:
        """Computes Population Stability Index (PSI) between baseline and deployment samples."""
        edges = np.quantile(baseline, np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, bins + 1))
        edges[0], edges[-1] = -np.inf, np.inf
        p = np.clip(np.histogram(baseline, bins=edges)[0] / len(baseline), 1e-6, 1.0)
        q = np.clip(np.histogram(current, bins=edges)[0] / len(current), 1e-6, 1.0)
        return float(np.sum((p - q) * np.log(p / q)))

    @staticmethod
    def generate_pipeline_digest(code: str, config: dict, seed: int) -> str:
        payload = {
            "code_hash": hashlib.sha256(code.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest(),
            "config": config,
            "seed": seed,
        }
        return hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(payload, sort_keys=True).encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()


def test_leakage_sentinel_assertions():
    np.random.seed(42)
    n = 1000

    # Synthetic credit loan dataset
    timestamps = pd.date_range("2024-01-01", periods=n, freq="h")
    credit_score = np.random.normal(680.0, 50.0, size=n)
    loan_amount = np.random.exponential(15000.0, size=n)
    default = (credit_score < 620.0).astype(int)

    # Type I Target Leak: 'recovery_fee' only recorded post-default!
    recovery_fee = np.zeros(n)
    recovery_fee[default == 1] = np.random.uniform(200.0, 1500.0, size=int(default.sum()))

    df = pd.DataFrame({
        "timestamp": timestamps,
        "credit_score": credit_score,
        "loan_amount": loan_amount,
        "recovery_fee": recovery_fee,
    })
    y = pd.Series(default, name="default")

    # Random shuffle split injects Type III Temporal Look-ahead!
    train_idx = np.random.choice(n, size=800, replace=False)
    test_idx = np.setdiff1d(np.arange(n), train_idx)
    X_train, y_train = df.iloc[train_idx].copy(), y.iloc[train_idx]
    X_test = df.iloc[test_idx].copy()

    # Inject 1 cross-partition duplicate (Type II Leak)
    X_test.iloc[0, 1:] = X_train.iloc[0, 1:]

    sentinel = DataLeakageSentinel(mi_threshold=0.30)

    # Audit Type I, II, III
    proxies = sentinel.audit_target_proxies(X_train[["loan_amount", "recovery_fee"]], y_train)
    dup_leaks = sentinel.audit_split_contamination(X_train.drop(columns=["timestamp"]), X_test.drop(columns=["timestamp"]))
    temp_leak = sentinel.audit_temporal_leakage(X_train["timestamp"], X_test["timestamp"])

    assert "recovery_fee" in proxies, "Type I Sentinel missed causal post-event proxy!"
    assert dup_leaks >= 1, "Type II Sentinel missed train-test duplicate row!"
    assert temp_leak is True, "Type III Sentinel missed temporal inversion!"

    # PSI Drift Check
    base_dist = np.random.normal(0.0, 1.0, size=2000)
    stable_dist = np.random.normal(0.0, 1.0, size=2000)
    drifted_dist = np.random.normal(0.8, 1.2, size=2000)
    psi_ok = sentinel.compute_psi(base_dist, stable_dist)
    psi_bad = sentinel.compute_psi(base_dist, drifted_dist)

    # Lineage Hash Check
    d1 = sentinel.generate_pipeline_digest("clean()", {"scaler": "robust"}, 42)
    d2 = sentinel.generate_pipeline_digest("clean()", {"scaler": "robust"}, 43)
    assert d1 != d2 and len(d1) == 64

    report = LeakageSentinelReport(
        type1_proxy_features=list(proxies.keys()),
        type2_split_duplicates_found=dup_leaks,
        type3_temporal_leak_detected=temp_leak,
        psi_stable_score=round(psi_ok, 4),
        psi_drifted_score=round(psi_bad, 4),
        sha256_lineage_digest=d1,
    )

    print("=== Production Data Leakage Sentinel & Governance Audit ===")
    print(f"Type I Causal Proxies Detected: {report.type1_proxy_features} (MI: {proxies})")
    print(f"Type II Cross-Split Duplicates: {report.type2_split_duplicates_found}")
    print(f"Type III Temporal Inversion Detected: {report.type3_temporal_leak_detected}")
    print(f"PSI Drift Sentinel -> Stable={report.psi_stable_score} vs Alert Triggered={report.psi_drifted_score}")
    print(f"Cryptographic SHA-256 Digest: {report.sha256_lineage_digest[:24]}...")
    print("✓ All Pydantic V2 governance schemas & sentinel alerts verified cleanly!")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    test_leakage_sentinel_assertions()

    6. Key Engineering Takeaways

    1. Eliminate Causal Reversal (Y -> X): Screen suspicious high-Mutual-Information features against creation timestamps; exclude post-outcome administrative, recovery, or billing artifacts.
    2. Enforce Temporal Filtrations: Random cross-validation splits on chronological event streams violate causality (\(\mathcal{F}_t\)); always deploy strictly ordered rolling/out-of-time splits.
    3. Automate PSI Drift Circuit Breakers: Continuous Population Stability Index tracking flags both genuine economic shifts (\(\text{PSI} > 0.25\)) and silent upstream schema mutations before defective predictions harm users.
    4. Seal Pipelines with Cryptographic Provenance: Bind every transformation pipeline run to immutable SHA-256 commit, configuration, and random seed digests to enforce regulatory compliance (GDPR Article 17).

    7. Scenario-Based Diagnostic Micro-Checks

    Micro-Check 1: Population Stability Index (PSI) Threshold Interpretation

    A credit underwriting pipeline computes monthly PSI against training baseline deciles for borrower DebtToIncomeRatio. In Month 4, PSI spikes to \(\text{PSI} = 0.34\). What are the two distinct engineering root causes that must be checked before retraining?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Any \(\text{PSI} > 0.25\) triggers a mandatory Circuit Breaker audit separating Data Pipeline Corruption from Genuine Distribution Drift:

    1. Upstream Data Schema Corruption: Verify that the data warehouse did not silently alter units (e.g., recording percentage 0.35 as 35.0), drop denominator incomes to NULL, or introduce duplicate rows. Never retrain a model on broken upstream data!
    2. Genuine Macro-Economic Shift: If schema assertions pass, the shift reflects real behavioral change (interest rate shock, inflation). Trigger an automated training refresh using calibrated validation weights.
    Micro-Check 2: Covariate Shift vs. Concept Drift

    Compare the mathematical conditional probability definitions of Covariate Shift and Concept Drift, providing a concrete financial example of each.

    Reveal Detailed Solution
    • Covariate Shift (\(P(X)\) shifts; \(P(Y \mid X)\) stays invariant): $\(P_{\text{train}}(X) \ne P_{\text{deploy}}(X), \qquad P_{\text{train}}(Y \mid X) = P_{\text{deploy}}(Y \mid X)\)$ Example: A digital bank shifts marketing from college campuses to retirees, altering customer age/wealth inputs \(X\), while default likelihood for any fixed borrower profile stays unchanged. Importance weighting (\(\frac{P_{\text{deploy}}(X)}{P_{\text{train}}(X)}\)) resolves this cleanly.
    • Concept Drift (\(P(Y \mid X)\) shifts): $\(P_{\text{train}}(Y \mid X) \ne P_{\text{deploy}}(Y \mid X)\)$ Example: A sudden pandemic alters fraudsters' patterns and consumer delinquency rules such that identical borrower attributes predict totally different default probabilities. Full fresh label acquisition and retraining are mandatory.
    Micro-Check 3: Why Random Cross-Validation Fails Silently on Time-Series Credit Streams

    An e-commerce marketplace generates \(100{,}000\) monthly orders across 2023–2024. Why does evaluating a customer LTV predictor with random 5-fold CV leak inflation metrics across folds?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Random row shuffling mixes future macroeconomic surges, holiday pricing shocks, and later transactions from the same customers into the training partition of earlier transactions.

    Evaluating on an order from March 2023 while the training fold already saw that same customer's November 2023 Black Friday purchases violates temporal filtration \(\mathcal{F}_t\). Production backtesting requires Out-of-Time Group Splits (\(t_{\text{train}} \le T_{\text{cutoff}} < t_{\text{test}}\)).

    Micro-Check 4: Provenance Semirings & GDPR Article 17 Lineage Auditing

    Under GDPR "Right to Erasure", a customer demands deletion of all records derived from their PII. How does Green et al.'s Provenance Semiring algebra identify which aggregated analytics tables require purging?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Semirings attach symbolic lineage polynomials \(\Phi(t) \in \mathbb{K}[X]\) using \(\oplus\) (alternative union sources) and \(\otimes\) (mandatory join inputs).

    When user token \(x_u\) is purged (\(x_u \leftarrow 0\) in semiring assignment): 1. Joined output tuples bearing monomial \(x_u \otimes x_k\) evaluate strictly to \(0\) (deleted immediately!). 2. Deduped or unioned outputs carrying polynomial \(x_u \oplus x_{\text{other}}\) drop \(x_u\) yet survive cleanly via alternative provenance factor \(x_{\text{other}}\), eliminating manual trial-and-error database scanning!
