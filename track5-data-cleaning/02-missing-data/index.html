    Missing values are ubiquitous across industrial data pipelines, clinical trials, and telemetry stores. Naive remediation shortcuts—such as zero-filling, univariate mean/median imputation, or complete-case listwise deletion—destroy feature variance, attenuate cross-feature correlations, and inject catastrophic selection bias [Rubin1976, Little2019]. Rigorous missingness engineering requires diagnosing the stochastic mechanism generating the missingness mask, testing ignorability assumptions, and deploying iterative multivariate imputation [VanBuuren2018, Fellegi1976].

    1. Conceptual Overview: Rubin's Missingness Taxonomy

    Donald Rubin established the mathematical hierarchy classifying how the probability of a missing entry depends on the underlying values of a dataset:

    2. Mathematical Taxonomy & Statistical Testing

    Let complete data matrix \(\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}\) be partitioned into observed entries \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}\) and missing entries \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{mis}}\). Define the binary missingness mask matrix \(\mathbf{M} \in \{0, 1\}^{N \times D}\), where \(M_{ij} = 1\) if \(Y_{ij}\) is missing and \(M_{ij} = 0\) if observed [Little2019].

    1. Missing Completely at Random (MCAR)

    Missingness is strictly independent of both observed and unobserved values:

    \[ \mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{M} \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}, \mathbf{Y}_{\text{mis}}, \boldsymbol{\psi}\right) = \mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{M} \;\middle|\; \boldsymbol{\psi}\right) \]

    Example: A clinical nurse accidentally drops a blood sample tube on the floor, or an IoT sensor experiences random network packet collisions uncorrelated with ambient temperature. Complete-case listwise deletion remains unbiased, though statistical power diminishes.

    2. Missing at Random (MAR)

    Missingness depends systematically on observed attributes \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}\), but is conditionally independent of unobserved missing values \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{mis}}\) once \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}\) is known:

    \[ \mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{M} \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}, \mathbf{Y}_{\text{mis}}, \boldsymbol{\psi}\right) = \mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{M} \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}, \boldsymbol{\psi}\right) \]

    Example: Male respondents (recorded in observed Gender column) are statistically less likely to complete an optional depression questionnaire than female respondents, irrespective of their true depression severity. Under MAR, listwise deletion causes severe selection bias, whereas likelihood-based MICE imputation is unbiased and ignorable.

    3. Missing Not at Random (MNAR / Non-Ignorable)

    Missingness depends directly on the unobserved missing values themselves, even after conditioning on all observable covariates:

    \[ \mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{M} \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}, \mathbf{Y}_{\text{mis}}, \boldsymbol{\psi}\right) \ne \mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{M} \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}, \boldsymbol{\psi}\right) \]

    Example: High-net-worth billionaires refuse to disclose their exact annual earnings on tax surveys because their income is extraordinarily high. No observed covariate fully absorbs the propensity to withhold. MNAR is non-ignorable: standard MICE alone cannot recover true population parameters without auxiliary Heckman Selection Models or Pattern-Mixture \(\delta\)-Sensitivity Adjustments.

    4. Little's MCAR Chi-Squared Test

    To formally test the null hypothesis \(H_0: \text{Missingness is MCAR}\), Roderick Little [Little2019] derived a global multivariate Wald-type \(\chi^2\) test across \(J\) distinct missing-data patterns:

    \[ d_{\text{MCAR}}^2 = \sum_{j=1}^J n_j \left( \bar{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{obs}, j} - \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_j \right)^T \widetilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_j^{-1} \left( \bar{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{obs}, j} - \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_j \right) \]

    Where \(n_j\) is the sample count of pattern \(j\), \(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{obs}, j}\) is the observed sample mean vector for pattern \(j\), and \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_j, \widetilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_j\) are the global Expectation-Maximization (EM) maximum-likelihood mean and covariance projections evaluated over the observed dimensions \(d_j\) of pattern \(j\). Under \(H_0\), \(d_{\text{MCAR}}^2 \sim \chi^2_{df}\) with degrees of freedom \(df = \sum_{j=1}^J d_j - D\). If \(p \le 0.05\), MCAR is rejected.

    5. Multivariate Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) & PMM

    MICE (also termed Fully Conditional Specification) handles arbitrary multivariate MAR patterns via iterative Gibbs sampling across features [VanBuuren2018]. At iteration \(t\):

    \[ \boldsymbol{\theta}_j^{(t)} \sim P\left( \boldsymbol{\theta}_j \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{j, \text{obs}}, \mathbf{Y}_{-j}^{(t-1)} \right) \]
    \[ \mathbf{Y}_{j, \text{mis}}^{(t)} \sim P\left( \mathbf{Y}_{j, \text{mis}} \;\middle|\; \mathbf{Y}_{-j}^{(t-1)}, \boldsymbol{\theta}_j^{(t)} \right) \]

    Predictive Mean Matching (PMM) Safeguard

    Standard linear regression imputation predicts exact expectations \(\hat{y}_i = \mathbf{x}_i^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\), which can generate impossible out-of-range values (e.g., negative blood pressure or fractional pregnancy counts) and underestimates residual variance. Predictive Mean Matching (PMM) identifies the \(k \in \{3, 5, 10\}\) observed donor cases whose predicted values \(\hat{y}_d\) are closest to \(\hat{y}_i\), and draws one actual observed value uniformly from that donor pool:

    \[ \text{Donor Set } \mathcal{D}(i) = \arg\min_{d \in \text{obs}, |\mathcal{D}| = k} \left| \mathbf{x}_i^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} - \mathbf{x}_d^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \right| \]

    PMM automatically guarantees domain plausibility, integer discreteness, and skewness preservation!

    6. Rubin's Pooling Rules & Fraction of Missing Information (FMI)

    Single imputation treats synthetic filled-in values as real ground-truth data, artificially shrinking standard errors and producing overconfident p-values. Multiple Imputation generates \(M\) independent imputed corpora (\(M \approx 5 \text{ to } 20\)).

    For target parameter estimate \(\hat{Q}_m\) and sampling variance \(U_m\) calculated in copy \(m\):

    \[ \bar{Q} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \hat{Q}_m \qquad \text{(Pooled Point Estimate)} \]
    \[ \bar{U} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M U_m \qquad \text{(Within-Imputation Sampling Variance)} \]
    \[ B = \frac{1}{M - 1} \sum_{m=1}^M \left( \hat{Q}_m - \bar{Q} \right)^2 \qquad \text{(Between-Imputation Uncertainty)} \]

    Total pooled variance \(T\) integrates both uncertainty components with finite-\(M\) correction:

    \[ T = \bar{U} + \left( 1 + \frac{1}{M} \right) B \]

    The Relative Increase in Variance due to Nonresponse (\(r_{\text{RIV}}\)) and Fraction of Missing Information (\(\gamma_{\text{FMI}}\)) quantify how severely missingness inflates parameter uncertainty:

    \[ r_{\text{RIV}} = \frac{\left( 1 + \frac{1}{M} \right) B}{\bar{U}}, \qquad \gamma_{\text{FMI}} = \frac{r_{\text{RIV}} + \frac{2}{\nu + 3}}{r_{\text{RIV}} + 1} \approx \frac{\left( 1 + \frac{1}{M} \right) B}{T} \]

    5. Production-Grade Python Verification Engine

    Below is a type-safe Python benchmark using Pydantic V2 that tests Listwise Deletion vs. Mean Imputation vs. Iterative MICE under MAR missingness and computes Rubin's Multiple Imputation pooling statistics.

    """
production_missing_data_benchmark.py
Pydantic V2 verified engine for MICE Imputation, Variance Preservation, and Rubin's Pooling.
"""

from typing import Dict, Any, List
import math
import numpy as np
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from sklearn.experimental import enable_iterative_imputer
from sklearn.impute import IterativeImputer, SimpleImputer


class ImputationBenchmarkResult(BaseModel):
    """Pydantic V2 schema for validating missing-data imputation quality."""
    true_variance: float
    true_correlation: float
    mean_imp_variance: float
    mean_imp_correlation: float
    mice_imp_variance: float
    mice_imp_correlation: float
    var_retention_mean_ratio: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.5)
    var_retention_mice_ratio: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.5)


class RubinPoolingMetrics(BaseModel):
    """Pydantic V2 schema for Rubin's Multiple Imputation pooling calculations."""
    num_imputations: int = Field(..., ge=2)
    pooled_estimate: float
    within_variance_u_bar: float = Field(..., ge=0.0)
    between_variance_b: float = Field(..., ge=0.0)
    total_variance_t: float
    fraction_missing_info: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.0)
    relative_increase_variance: float = Field(..., ge=0.0)

    @classmethod
    def from_estimates(cls, q_estimates: List[float], u_variances: List[float]) -> "RubinPoolingMetrics":
        M = len(q_estimates)
        q_bar = float(np.mean(q_estimates))
        u_bar = float(np.mean(u_variances))
        b = float(np.var(q_estimates, ddof=1))
        total_t = u_bar + (1.0 + 1.0 / M) * b
        riv = ((1.0 + 1.0 / M) * b) / u_bar if u_bar > 0 else 0.0
        fmi = ((1.0 + 1.0 / M) * b) / total_t if total_t > 0 else 0.0
        return cls(
            num_imputations=M,
            pooled_estimate=round(q_bar, 4),
            within_variance_u_bar=round(u_bar, 4),
            between_variance_b=round(b, 4),
            total_variance_t=round(total_t, 4),
            fraction_missing_info=round(fmi, 4),
            relative_increase_variance=round(riv, 4),
        )


def run_imputation_benchmark(X_clean: np.ndarray, X_missing: np.ndarray) -> ImputationBenchmarkResult:
    true_var = float(np.var(X_clean[:, 0]))
    true_corr = float(np.corrcoef(X_clean[:, 0], X_clean[:, 1])[0, 1])

    # Naive Mean Imputation
    mean_imp = SimpleImputer(strategy="mean")
    X_mean = mean_imp.fit_transform(X_missing)
    mean_var = float(np.var(X_mean[:, 0]))
    mean_corr = float(np.corrcoef(X_mean[:, 0], X_mean[:, 1])[0, 1])

    # Multivariate Chained Equations (MICE) with BayesianRidge posterior sampling
    mice_imp = IterativeImputer(max_iter=20, sample_posterior=True, random_state=42)
    X_mice = mice_imp.fit_transform(X_missing)
    mice_var = float(np.var(X_mice[:, 0]))
    mice_corr = float(np.corrcoef(X_mice[:, 0], X_mice[:, 1])[0, 1])

    return ImputationBenchmarkResult(
        true_variance=round(true_var, 4),
        true_correlation=round(true_corr, 4),
        mean_imp_variance=round(mean_var, 4),
        mean_imp_correlation=round(mean_corr, 4),
        mice_imp_variance=round(mice_var, 4),
        mice_imp_correlation=round(mice_corr, 4),
        var_retention_mean_ratio=round(mean_var / true_var, 4),
        var_retention_mice_ratio=round(mice_var / true_var, 4),
    )


def test_missing_data_assertions():
    np.random.seed(42)
    N = 2500

    # Bivariate correlated Gaussian: r = 0.75
    mean = [50.0, 100.0]
    cov = [[25.0, 30.0], [30.0, 64.0]]
    X_clean = np.random.multivariate_normal(mean, cov, size=N)

    # Inject strong MAR missingness on X[:, 0] conditioned on X[:, 1]
    X_missing = X_clean.copy()
    probs = 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-0.10 * (X_clean[:, 1] - 100.0)))
    mask = np.random.rand(N) < probs
    X_missing[mask, 0] = np.nan

    res = run_imputation_benchmark(X_clean, X_missing)

    # Assertions
    assert res.var_retention_mean_ratio < 0.70, f"Expected severe mean deflation, got {res.var_retention_mean_ratio}"
    assert res.var_retention_mice_ratio > 0.90, f"Expected MICE to preserve variance (>0.90), got {res.var_retention_mice_ratio}"
    assert abs(res.mice_imp_correlation - res.true_correlation) < abs(res.mean_imp_correlation - res.true_correlation)

    # Rubin's Pooling Rules Test
    q_ests = [10.2, 10.5, 9.8, 10.1, 10.4]
    u_vars = [4.0, 4.1, 3.9, 4.0, 4.0]
    pooled = RubinPoolingMetrics.from_estimates(q_ests, u_vars)
    assert pooled.total_variance_t > pooled.within_variance_u_bar
    assert 0.0 < pooled.fraction_missing_info < 0.15

    print("=== Production Missing Data & Rubin Pooling Report ===")
    print(f"Variance Retention (Mean Imp): {res.var_retention_mean_ratio*100:.1f}%")
    print(f"Variance Retention (MICE Imp): {res.var_retention_mice_ratio*100:.1f}%")
    print(f"Correlation Error (Mean vs MICE): {abs(res.mean_imp_correlation - res.true_correlation):.4f} vs {abs(res.mice_imp_correlation - res.true_correlation):.4f}")
    print(f"Rubin Pooled T={pooled.total_variance_t} (FMI={pooled.fraction_missing_info*100:.1f}%)")
    print("✓ All Pydantic V2 schemas and statistical invariants verified successfully!")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    test_missing_data_assertions()

from typing import Dict, Any

def impute_with_evaluation(X_clean: np.ndarray, X_missing: np.ndarray, strategy: str = "mean") -> Dict[str, float]:
    """
    Imputes missing values in X_missing (2D numpy array with NaNs) and benchmarks variance retention against X_clean.

    Parameters:
      X_clean: Ground-truth uncorrupted 2D array of shape (N, D)
      X_missing: Corrupted 2D array containing np.nan entries
      strategy: "mean" or "median"

    Returns:
      dict with:
        - "true_variance": float (variance of column 0 in X_clean)
        - "imputed_variance": float (variance of column 0 in imputed array)
        - "variance_retention": float (imputed_variance / true_variance)
    """
    X_imputed = X_missing.copy()

    if strategy == "mean":
        col_stats = np.nanmean(X_imputed, axis=0)
    elif strategy == "median":
        col_stats = np.nanmedian(X_imputed, axis=0)
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unsupported strategy: {strategy}")

    for col_idx in range(X_imputed.shape[1]):
        mask = np.isnan(X_imputed[:, col_idx])
        X_imputed[mask, col_idx] = col_stats[col_idx]

    true_var = float(np.var(X_clean[:, 0]))
    imp_var = float(np.var(X_imputed[:, 0]))
    retention = imp_var / true_var if true_var != 0 else 1.0

    return {
        "true_variance": round(true_var, 4),
        "imputed_variance": round(imp_var, 4),
        "variance_retention": round(retention, 4)
    }

    7. Scenario-Based Diagnostic Micro-Checks

    Micro-Check 1: Rubin's Variance Pooling & FMI Calculation

    In a clinical prognostic study with \(M = 5\) multiple imputations: - Average within-imputation sampling variance: \(\bar{U} = 4.0\) - Between-imputation variance across imputed datasets: \(B = 1.5\) Calculate the total pooled variance \(T\), Relative Increase in Variance \(r_{\text{RIV}}\), and approximate Fraction of Missing Information (\(\gamma_{\text{FMI}}\)).

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Total pooled variance is \(T = 5.80\), \(r_{\text{RIV}} = 45.0\%\), and \(\gamma_{\text{FMI}} = 31.0\%\).

    1. Total Pooled Variance (\(T\)): $\(T = \bar{U} + \left(1 + \frac{1}{M}\right) B = 4.0 + \left(1 + \frac{1}{5}\right) \times 1.5 = 4.0 + 1.2 \times 1.5 = 5.80\)$
    2. Relative Increase in Variance (\(r_{\text{RIV}}\)): $\(r_{\text{RIV}} = \frac{(1 + 1/M) B}{\bar{U}} = \frac{1.80}{4.0} = 0.45 \implies 45.0\%\)$
    3. Fraction of Missing Information (\(\gamma_{\text{FMI}}\)): $\(\gamma_{\text{FMI}} \approx \frac{(1 + 1/M) B}{T} = \frac{1.80}{5.80} \approx 0.3103 \implies 31.0\%\)$ Roughly \(31\%\) of total regression parameter uncertainty originates directly from missing observations rather than finite population size.
    Micro-Check 2: Why Mean Imputation Destroys Covariance & Variance

    Suppose feature \(X\) has variance \(\sigma_x^2 = 100\), and \(30\%\) of its values are missing completely at random (MCAR). If an engineer replaces all missing cells with the sample mean \(\bar{x}_{\text{obs}}\), what happens to the variance \(\sigma_x^2\) and Pearson correlation \(r_{xy}\)?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Feature variance shrinks linearly by the missing fraction (\(30\%\) drop to \(\sigma^2 \approx 70\)), and bivariate correlation collapses proportionally.

    When a fraction \(p_{\text{miss}}\) of entries in \(X\) are set to the constant \(\bar{x}\): 1. Variance Collapse: Every imputed entry has deviation \((x_i - \bar{x}) = 0\), contributing zero squared deviations to the numerator: $\(\text{Var}(X_{\text{mean\_imp}}) \approx (1 - p_{\text{miss}}) \cdot \sigma_x^2 = 0.70 \times 100 = 70\)$ 2. Covariance Attenuation: In Pearson correlation \(r_{xy} = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}\), the numerator loses \(30\%\) of all co-varying signal pairs while \(Y\) maintains its un-shrunk variance. This causes \(r_{xy}\) to shrink strictly toward \(0\), blinding downstream models to real relationships.

    Micro-Check 3: Predictive Mean Matching (PMM) vs. Pure Regression

    A healthcare pipeline fills in missing daily patient Cigarettes_Smoked counts using ordinary linear regression imputation conditioned on age and respiratory symptoms. Downstream audits discover records showing -4.37 cigarettes per day and 3.82 cigarettes. How does Predictive Mean Matching (PMM) resolve both anomalies?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    PMM restricts imputed draws strictly to existing empirical donor values observed in the population.

    Pure parametric linear regression predicts continuous unbounded expectations \(\hat{y}_i = \mathbf{x}_i^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\), ignoring bounded domains (\(y \ge 0\)) and integer discreteness.

    Under PMM: 1. Linear regression predicts \(\hat{y}_i = -4.37\) solely as a distance matching metric. 2. The algorithm locates the \(k=5\) observed patients with the lowest predicted values \(\hat{y}_d\) (whose actual recorded values are valid integers like 0, 0, 1, 0, 0). 3. One donor is sampled uniformly at random, guaranteeing that imputed outputs can never violate non-negativity constraints, fractional bounds, or skewed distributional boundaries.

    Micro-Check 4: Diagnosing MNAR Self-Censoring vs. MAR in Surveys

    In an anonymized customer satisfaction survey, \(45\%\) of dissatisfied customers who suffered prolonged tech-support outages drop out before rating their experience (Rating = NULL). Observed support ticket logs document that those customers waited over 90 minutes on hold. Is this missingness mechanism MCAR, MAR, or MNAR? What if outage logs were never collected?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    With outage wait-time logs present in \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}\), it is MAR; without outage logs, it becomes MNAR.

    1. With Auxiliary Logs (MAR): If hold times and ticket escalations are faithfully recorded in observed covariates \(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{obs}}\), conditioning MICE on wait time absorbs the propensity to drop out. Within any fixed hold-time stratum, the dropout decision becomes independent of the exact numerical rating.
    2. Without Auxiliary Logs (MNAR): If wait times were discarded and no observed feature explains why customers dropped out, then Rating missingness correlates directly with the unobserved negative Rating itself. Listwise deletion or naive MICE would artificially inflate average customer satisfaction scores.
