    🎯 Conceptual Overview & Bias Taxonomy

    Dataset bias is not an amorphous, singular flaw; it is a family of distinct structural distortions entering the machine learning pipeline at identifiable phases of the data lifecycle [Mehrabi2021, Barocas2016, Torralba2011]. Auditing and mitigating bias requires establishing a precise mathematical taxonomy that separates societal realities, sampling selection functions, sensor measurement errors, and statistical aggregation artifacts.

    1. Mathematical Taxonomy of Dataset Biases

    1. Historical Bias (World-to-Data Divergence)

    Historical bias occurs when the data-generation process accurately and faithfully reflects existing socio-economic disparities, systemic prejudices, or historical inequities:

    \[\Pr_{\text{world}}(Y = 1 \mid A = 0) \neq \Pr_{\text{world}}(Y = 1 \mid A = 1)\]

    Even if sampling, measurement, and annotation are mathematically perfect, an empirical risk minimizer optimizes for historical status-quo reproduction rather than normative equity.

    2. Sampling Selection Bias & Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW)

    Selection bias occurs when the probability of an observation being selected into the training dataset (\(S = 1\)) is conditionally dependent on the features \(\mathbf{X}\) or protected attribute \(A\):

    \[\Pr(S = 1 \mid \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x}, A = a) \neq \Pr(S = 1)\]

    Let \(\pi(\mathbf{x}, a) = \Pr(S = 1 \mid \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x}, A = a) > 0\) denote the propensity of selection. The empirical distribution of sampled data \(P_{\text{sample}}(\mathbf{X})\) diverges from the population \(P_{\text{pop}}(\mathbf{X})\).

    To recover unbiased population estimates of any target parameter \(\mu_Y = \mathbb{E}[Y]\), we apply the Horvitz-Thompson Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) Estimator:

    \[\hat{\mu}_{\text{HT}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{S_i Y_i}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)}\]
    Unbiasedness Proof of Horvitz-Thompson IPW

    Taking the expectation over the joint distribution of \((S, \mathbf{X}, A, Y)\):

    \[\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}_{\text{HT}}] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{S_i Y_i}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)} \right] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}, A, Y} \left[ \frac{\mathbb{E}[S_i \mid \mathbf{X}_i, A_i, Y_i] Y_i}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)} \right]\]

    Under the missing-at-random (MAR) assumption, \(\mathbb{E}[S_i \mid \mathbf{X}_i, A_i, Y_i] = \Pr(S_i = 1 \mid \mathbf{X}_i, A_i) = \pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)\):

    \[\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mu}_{\text{HT}}] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}, A, Y} \left[ \frac{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i) Y_i}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)} \right] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{E}[Y_i] = \mu_Y \quad \blacksquare\]

    In practice, the self-normalized Hajek Estimator is preferred due to reduced finite-sample variance:

    \[\hat{\mu}_{\text{Hajek}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{S_i Y_i}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)}}{\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{S_i}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_i, A_i)}}\]

    3. Measurement Error: Classical vs. Differential Attenuation

    When predictors \(\mathbf{X}\) are recorded through physical sensors, survey instruments, or administrative databases, measurement noise distorts the relationship with target \(Y\).

    Classical Measurement Error & Regression Dilution (Attenuation Bias)

    Let true predictor \(X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_X^2)\) be unobservable. Instead, we observe \(X^* = X + u\), where \(u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_u^2)\) is zero-mean noise independent of \(X\) and regression disturbance \(\epsilon\). The true underlying data-generating process is:

    \[Y = \beta X + \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\epsilon^2)\]

    When Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regresses \(Y\) onto the noisy measurement \(X^*\), the estimated slope is:

    \[\hat{\beta} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X^*, Y)}{\text{Var}(X^*)} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X + u, \beta X + \epsilon)}{\text{Var}(X + u)}\]

    Because \(u \perp X\), \(u \perp \epsilon\), and \(X \perp \epsilon\):

    \[\text{Cov}(X + u, \beta X + \epsilon) = \beta \text{Cov}(X, X) + \text{Cov}(X, \epsilon) + \beta \text{Cov}(u, X) + \text{Cov}(u, \epsilon) = \beta \sigma_X^2\]
    \[\text{Var}(X + u) = \sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2\]

    Substituting gives the classical attenuation formula:

    \[\hat{\beta} = \beta \cdot \left( \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2} \right) = \beta \cdot \lambda_{\text{rel}}\]

    where \(\lambda_{\text{rel}} = \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2} \in (0, 1]\) is the reliability ratio. The estimated relationship is strictly attenuated (shrunk towards zero).

    Differential Measurement Error Across Demographic Groups

    In socio-technical systems, measurement error is rarely classical; sensor accuracy depends directly on demographic attributes \(A\):

    \[\sigma_{u \mid A = 0}^2 \gg \sigma_{u \mid A = 1}^2\]

    Consequently, group-specific reliability ratios diverge:

    \[\lambda_0 = \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_{u \mid A = 0}^2} \ll \lambda_1 = \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_{u \mid A = 1}^2} \approx 1.0\]
    • For privileged Group 1, the model recovers \(\hat{\beta}_1 \approx \beta\).
    • For unprivileged Group 0, the model estimates \(\hat{\beta}_0 = \beta \cdot \lambda_0 \ll \beta\).

    [!WARNING] The Algorithmic Consequence of Differential Attenuation: An automated scoring system will perceive the feature \(X\) as substantially less predictive or reliable for Group 0. In multi-feature models, the estimator will discard or downweight \(X\) for Group 0 in favor of spurious demographic correlates, driving predictive inequality [Obermeyer2019].

    4. Proxy Label Bias & The Obermeyer Health Disparity

    Proxy label bias occurs when the true target concept \(Y^*\) is unobservable or legally restricted, forcing engineers to substitute an accessible operational proxy \(\tilde{Y}\):

    \[\tilde{Y} = g(Y^*, A) + \eta\]

    In 2019, Obermeyer et al. uncovered a massive commercial healthcare algorithm affecting \(>200\) million patients [Obermeyer2019]: - Target Concept: True healthcare need \(Y^*\) (number of active chronic conditions, severity of heart failure, diabetes). - Engineered Proxy: Total healthcare annual costs \(\tilde{Y}\). - Structural Distortion: Due to unequal access to healthcare, insurance disparities, and discriminatory physician utilization patterns, less money was historically spent on Black patients relative to White patients at identical illness levels: $\(\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y} \mid Y^* = y, A = \text{Black}] < \mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y} \mid Y^* = y, A = \text{White}]\)$ - Result: To qualify for specialized high-risk care management programs (threshold \(\tilde{Y} > \tau\)), Black patients had to be significantly sicker than White patients. Remedying the proxy label bias boosted the proportion of Black patients enrolled from \(17.7\%\) to \(46.5\%\).

    5. Aggregation Bias & Simpson's Reversal in Loss Minimization

    Aggregation bias occurs when a single model is fitted across heterogeneous sub-populations where the true underlying conditional mapping varies by group:

    \[\mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathbf{X}, A = 0] \neq \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathbf{X}, A = 1]\]

    Consider two groups where within each group, feature \(X\) has a positive effect on outcome \(Y\) (\(\beta_0 > 0, \beta_1 > 0\)). If group centroids are offset such that the group with higher \(X\) has lower baseline intercept, a global pooled regression can produce a negative overall slope:

    \[\hat{\beta}_{\text{pooled}} = \frac{\sum_{i} (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sum_{i} (X_i - \bar{X})^2} < 0\]

    Fitting a pooled estimator minimizes average population risk, sacrificing the minority subgroup whose local conditional relationship is completely reversed by aggregate loss optimization.

    6. Intersectional Under-Representation & Gender Shades

    Representation bias scales exponentially worse across intersectional demographic identities [Buolamwini2018]. Let demographic attributes be decomposed into independent dimensions (e.g., gender \(G \in \{M, F\}\), skin phenotype \(P \in \{L, D\}\)). If collection achieves marginal balance:

    \[\Pr(G = F) = 0.50, \quad \Pr(P = D) = 0.50\]

    Convenience sampling often severely depletes the intersectional slice:

    \[\Pr(G = F, P = D) \ll 0.25\]

    In the landmark Gender Shades study by Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru [Buolamwini2018], commercial facial classification APIs achieved \(>99\%\) accuracy on lighter males, but error rates exploded to \(34.7\%\) on darker females.

    💻 Production Implementation & Pydantic V2 Verification Suite

    Below is an executable Python suite with Pydantic V2 validating the three key quantitative dataset bias phenomena: 1. Sampling Selection Bias & Horvitz-Thompson Recovery: Verifies unbiased population mean recovery via inverse probability weighting. 2. Measurement Error Attenuation: Verifies the closed-form reliability ratio \(\lambda = \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2}\) against empirical regression dilution. 3. Proxy Label Bias (Obermeyer Simulation): Demonstrates how substituting healthcare cost for clinical illness severely undercounts the health needs of minority patients.

# Production Implementation & Pydantic V2 Verification Suite for Lesson 7.2

from typing import Dict, List, Any
import numpy as np
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field

class SamplingBiasAudit(BaseModel):
    true_population_mean: float = Field(..., description="True population mean mu_Y")
    biased_sample_mean: float = Field(..., description="Unweighted sample mean under selection bias")
    ht_weighted_mean: float = Field(..., description="Horvitz-Thompson IPW reconstructed mean")
    hajek_weighted_mean: float = Field(..., description="Self-normalized Hajek IPW mean")
    unweighted_error: float
    ipw_error: float
    ipw_recovery_successful: bool

class MeasurementErrorAudit(BaseModel):
    true_beta: float = Field(..., description="True underlying structural slope")
    var_x: float = Field(..., description="Variance of true latent predictor X")
    var_u_group0: float = Field(..., description="Sensor noise variance for Group 0")
    var_u_group1: float = Field(..., description="Sensor noise variance for Group 1")
    theoretical_lambda_group0: float = Field(..., description="Reliability ratio for Group 0")
    theoretical_lambda_group1: float = Field(..., description="Reliability ratio for Group 1")
    empirical_slope_group0: float
    empirical_slope_group1: float
    group0_attenuation_pct: float
    attenuation_proven: bool

class ProxyLabelBiasAudit(BaseModel):
    target_enrolled_rate_privileged: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.0)
    target_enrolled_rate_unprivileged: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.0)
    mean_illness_enrolled_privileged: float
    mean_illness_enrolled_unprivileged: float
    illness_severity_disparity: float
    proxy_bias_confirmed: bool

def audit_sampling_selection() -> SamplingBiasAudit:
    np.random.seed(42)
    n = 60000
    x = np.random.normal(50.0, 10.0, n)
    y = 2.0 * x + np.random.normal(0, 5.0, n)
    pop_mean = float(np.mean(y))

    # Selection probability: pi(x) = 1 / (1 + exp(-(x - 50) / 8))
    pi = 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-(x - 50.0) / 8.0))
    s = (np.random.uniform(0, 1, n) < pi).astype(int)

    unweighted_mean = float(np.mean(y[s == 1]))
    ht_mean = float(np.sum((s * y) / pi) / n)
    hajek_mean = float(np.sum((s * y) / pi) / np.sum(s / pi))

    unw_err = abs(unweighted_mean - pop_mean)
    ipw_err = abs(hajek_mean - pop_mean)

    return SamplingBiasAudit(
        true_population_mean=round(pop_mean, 4),
        biased_sample_mean=round(unweighted_mean, 4),
        ht_weighted_mean=round(ht_mean, 4),
        hajek_weighted_mean=round(hajek_mean, 4),
        unweighted_error=round(unw_err, 4),
        ipw_error=round(ipw_err, 4),
        ipw_recovery_successful=(ipw_err < 0.10 and unw_err > 5.0)
    )

def audit_measurement_error() -> MeasurementErrorAudit:
    np.random.seed(42)
    n = 80000
    sigma_x = 1.5
    var_x = sigma_x ** 2
    true_beta = 2.40

    x = np.random.normal(0, sigma_x, n)
    y = true_beta * x + np.random.normal(0, 1.0, n)

    # Differential noise
    sigma_u0 = 2.60 # Noisy sensor
    var_u0 = sigma_u0 ** 2
    sigma_u1 = 0.30 # Clean sensor
    var_u1 = sigma_u1 ** 2

    x_star0 = x + np.random.normal(0, sigma_u0, n)
    x_star1 = x + np.random.normal(0, sigma_u1, n)

    lambda0 = var_x / (var_x + var_u0)
    lambda1 = var_x / (var_x + var_u1)

    slope0 = float(np.cov(x_star0, y)[0, 1] / np.var(x_star0))
    slope1 = float(np.cov(x_star1, y)[0, 1] / np.var(x_star1))

    att_pct = (1.0 - slope0 / true_beta) * 100.0
    match = abs(slope0 - (true_beta * lambda0)) < 0.05

    return MeasurementErrorAudit(
        true_beta=true_beta,
        var_x=round(var_x, 4),
        var_u_group0=round(var_u0, 4),
        var_u_group1=round(var_u1, 4),
        theoretical_lambda_group0=round(lambda0, 4),
        theoretical_lambda_group1=round(lambda1, 4),
        empirical_slope_group0=round(slope0, 4),
        empirical_slope_group1=round(slope1, 4),
        group0_attenuation_pct=round(att_pct, 2),
        attenuation_proven=match
    )

def audit_proxy_label_bias() -> ProxyLabelBiasAudit:
    np.random.seed(42)
    n = 20000
    # Group 0 (Black, 20%), Group 1 (White, 80%)
    group = np.random.choice([0, 1], size=n, p=[0.2, 0.8])
    true_illness = np.random.normal(3.0, 1.0, size=n)

    # Expenditure proxy: $4000 per illness unit for Group 1, but only $2600 for Group 0
    costs = np.where(group == 1, 4000.0 * true_illness, 2600.0 * true_illness) + np.random.normal(0, 400, size=n)

    # Healthcare management enrollment threshold: top 15% predicted by cost
    tau = np.percentile(costs, 85)
    enrolled = costs >= tau

    rate_0 = float(np.mean(enrolled[group == 0]))
    rate_1 = float(np.mean(enrolled[group == 1]))

    mean_ill_0 = float(np.mean(true_illness[enrolled & (group == 0)]))
    mean_ill_1 = float(np.mean(true_illness[enrolled & (group == 1)]))

    return ProxyLabelBiasAudit(
        target_enrolled_rate_privileged=round(rate_1, 4),
        target_enrolled_rate_unprivileged=round(rate_0, 4),
        mean_illness_enrolled_privileged=round(mean_ill_1, 2),
        mean_illness_enrolled_unprivileged=round(mean_ill_0, 2),
        illness_severity_disparity=round(mean_ill_0 - mean_ill_1, 2),
        proxy_bias_confirmed=(rate_0 < 0.05 and (mean_ill_0 - mean_ill_1) > 1.5)
    )

    🔬 Empirical Case Studies: Physical & Algorithmic Reality

    1. Pulse Oximeter Optical Hypoxemia Disparity in COVID-19:
    2. Clinical Context: Pulse oximeters estimate arterial oxygen saturation (\(SpO_2\)) non-invasively by emitting red (\(660\,\text{nm}\)) and infrared (\(940\,\text{nm}\)) light through tissue.
    3. Differential Measurement Mechanism: The calibration curves connecting light absorption to oxygen saturation were fitted almost exclusively on fair-skinned European cohorts. Melanin absorbs light in the same optical window, introducing differential measurement error on patients with darker skin pigmentation.

    4. Clinical Consequence: The sensor systematically overestimated blood oxygen saturation on Black and Hispanic COVID-19 patients, causing occult hypoxemia (\(SaO_2 < 88\%\)) to go undetected at nearly triple the frequency of White patients, delaying supplemental oxygen therapy and emergency ICU admissions [Sjoding2020].

    5. Computer Vision Geographic & Cultural Benchmark Skew:

    6. The Dollar Street & ImageNet Audits: Shankar et al. (2017) and DeVries et al. (2019) demonstrated that \(>80\%\) of images in standard computer vision benchmarks (ImageNet, Open Images) originated from the United States and Europe.
    7. Representation Failure: State-of-the-art vision models recognized US wedding dresses and dish soap brands with \(>98\%\) accuracy, but failed completely on traditional Nigerian or Indian bridal attire, classifying them as "costumes" or "theatrical performers."

    ❓ Interactive Self-Assessment Micro-Checks

    Self-Assessment 1: Deriving the Reliability Ratio for Attenuation Bias

    In a simple linear regression \(Y = \beta X + \epsilon\) with \(X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_X^2)\), suppose \(X\) is measured through a noisy sensor \(X^* = X + u\) with \(u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_u^2)\) and \(u \perp X\). Prove why \(\text{plim} \; \hat{\beta}_{\text{OLS}} = \beta \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2}\).

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: The OLS estimator of slope is: $\(\hat{\beta} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i^* - \bar{X}^*)(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i^* - \bar{X}^*)^2}\)$ By the Weak Law of Large Numbers: $\(\text{plim} \; \hat{\beta} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X^*, Y)}{\text{Var}(X^*)} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X + u, \beta X + \epsilon)}{\text{Var}(X + u)}\)$ Expanding numerator covariance: $\(\text{Cov}(X + u, \beta X + \epsilon) = \beta \text{Cov}(X, X) + \text{Cov}(X, \epsilon) + \beta \text{Cov}(u, X) + \text{Cov}(u, \epsilon)\)$ Since disturbances \(\epsilon\) and sensor noise \(u\) are mutually independent of each other and of \(X\), the last three terms vanish to 0, leaving \(\beta \text{Var}(X) = \beta \sigma_X^2\).

    Expanding denominator variance: $\(\text{Var}(X + u) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(u) + 2\text{Cov}(X, u) = \sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2\)$ Therefore: $\(\text{plim} \; \hat{\beta} = \beta \cdot \left( \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\sigma_X^2 + \sigma_u^2} \right) = \beta \lambda_{\text{rel}} < \beta \quad \blacksquare\)$

    Self-Assessment 2: Unbiasedness of Horvitz-Thompson Estimator

    Why does standard sample mean \(\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\) fail under selection bias, and how does weighting by \(w_i = 1 / \pi_i\) restore unbiasedness?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: - Under selection bias, inclusion indicator \(S_i\) is correlated with \(Y_i\) (or covariates \(\mathbf{X}_i\) predictive of \(Y\)). Thus \(\mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid S_i = 1] \neq \mathbb{E}[Y_i]\), meaning the observed sample mean is systematically biased. - In the Horvitz-Thompson estimator \(\hat{\mu}_{\text{HT}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{S_i Y_i}{\pi_i}\), taking conditional expectations over \(S_i\) yields: $\(\mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{S_i Y_i}{\pi_i} \,\middle|\, \mathbf{X}_i, Y_i \right] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[S_i \mid \mathbf{X}_i, Y_i] Y_i}{\pi_i} = \frac{\pi_i Y_i}{\pi_i} = Y_i\)$ - Weighting each observed unit inversely by its sampling probability accounts for the underrepresented fraction of the target population that the unit represents.

    Self-Assessment 3: The Obermeyer Proxy Trap

    Suppose an automated hiring algorithm seeks to predict "Employee Productivity" \(Y^*\), but uses "Manager Performance Ratings" \(\tilde{Y}\) as a training target. Explain why this constitutes proxy label bias if female employees receive lower ratings for identical output due to subjective evaluator stereotypes.

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: - The true target is productive output \(Y^*\). The operational training label is \(\tilde{Y} = Y^* - \delta \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{Gender} = F) + \epsilon\), where \(\delta > 0\) reflects cognitive evaluator bias. - An ML model optimized to minimize loss on \(\tilde{Y}\) will internalize that female employees have lower expected labels for identical skill profiles: $\(\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y} \mid \mathbf{X}, F] < \mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y} \mid \mathbf{X}, M]\)$ - The model will actively discriminate against qualified female candidates, not because female employees are less productive, but because the engineered proxy conflated productivity with subjective human appraisal bias.

    Self-Assessment 4: Simpson's Paradox in Aggregation Bias

    Can an algorithm produce a negative predicted relationship between income and creditworthiness across the whole population if the relationship is positive within every single ethnic subgroup?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: - Yes. This is the classic definition of Simpson's Paradox (Aggregation Bias). - If Group 1 has high average income but faces a lower historical credit scale due to regional or baseline differences, while Group 0 has low average income but a higher baseline credit score, pooling the data into a single regression line connects the low-income/high-score cluster to the high-income/low-score cluster, generating a negative pooled slope. - Minimizing global empirical risk without group stratification forces the model to learn an inverse relationship that is false for all individuals within every subgroup.
