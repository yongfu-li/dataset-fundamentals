    🎯 Conceptual Overview & Causal Architecture

    In empirical research and large-scale data engineering, a Codebook maps raw numerical or symbolic encoding schemes to concrete qualitative semantics (e.g., mapping integer 99 to Refused to Answer rather than an exact physiological value) (Michener et al., 1997). Simultaneously, Data Provenance tracks the end-to-end historical transformations that turned raw inputs into downstream training splits (Moreau et al., 2011; Buneman et al., 2001). Together with Semantic Release Notes (SemVer 2.0.0), these artifacts form the auditable backbone of trustworthy data science (Herschel et al., 2017; Preston-Werner, 2013), preventing silent forensic failure modes such as the off-by-one column alignment disaster that forced trial cancellations in biomedical oncology (Baggerly & Coombes, 2009).

    📐 Mathematical Formulations & Theoretical Derivations

    To replace ad-hoc file spreadsheets with mathematically auditable data engineering, we formalize dataset lineage as directed graph reachability, semantic codebooks as categorical measure functions, and version transitions as deterministic state machines.

    Definition 8.3.1 (W3C PROV-O Bipartite Lineage Algebra)

    A dataset provenance graph is formalized as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) \(\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})\) conforming to the W3C PROV-O ontology (Moreau et al., 2011; Moreau & Missier, 2013):

    \[\mathcal{V} = \mathbf{E} \cup \mathbf{A} \cup \mathbf{Ag}\]

    where:

    • \(\mathbf{E}\) is the set of immutable data Entities (raw tables, normalized Parquet partitions, model checkpoints).
    • \(\mathbf{A}\) is the set of computational Activities (ETL jobs, top-coding transforms, SGD training steps).
    • \(\mathbf{Ag}\) is the set of responsible Agents (automated CI runners, Airflow schedulers, principal investigators).

    The directed edge set \(\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{V}\) encodes four canonical asymmetric causal dependencies (Buneman et al., 2001):

    • Generation: \(e \in \mathbf{E} \xrightarrow{\text{wasGeneratedBy}} a \in \mathbf{A}\) (or data-flow direction \(a \to e\) in execution DAGs)
    • Usage: \(a \in \mathbf{A} \xrightarrow{\text{used}} e \in \mathbf{E}\) (data-flow direction \(e \to a\))
    • Derivation: \(e_2 \in \mathbf{E} \xrightarrow{\text{wasDerivedFrom}} e_1 \in \mathbf{E}\)
    • Attribution / Association: \(e \in \mathbf{E} \xrightarrow{\text{wasAttributedTo}} ag \in \mathbf{Ag}\) and \(a \in \mathbf{A} \xrightarrow{\text{wasAssociatedWith}} ag \in \mathbf{Ag}\)

    Definition 8.3.2 (Nilpotent Transitive Reachability Matrix)

    Let vertices in the execution workflow be topologically ordered \([v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n]\), and let \(\mathbf{A} \in \{0, 1\}^{n \times n}\) denote the forward execution adjacency matrix of \(\mathcal{G}\), where \(\mathbf{A}_{ij} = 1\) if there exists a direct processing step \(v_i \to v_j\) (meaning artifact \(v_j\) directly consumes or is generated by \(v_i\)).

    The number of distinct directed derivation paths of length \(m\) from \(v_j\) to \(v_k\) equals \((\mathbf{A}^m)_{jk}\). Evaluating all path lengths yields the Transitive Reachability Matrix \(\mathcal{R} \in \{0, 1\}^{n \times n}\):

    \[\mathcal{R} = \mathbb{I}\left( \sum_{m=0}^\infty \mathbf{A}^m > 0 \right), \qquad \text{Ancestry}(v_k) = \left\{ v_j \in \mathcal{V} \;\middle|\; \mathcal{R}_{jk} = 1 \right\}\]

    Theorem 8.3.1 (Nilpotent Finite Convergence of PROV-O DAGs): Because every valid provenance graph \(\mathcal{G}\) is acyclic with maximum topological depth \(L = \text{depth}(\mathcal{G}) < n\), any product of \(m > L\) directed edges vanishes identically: \(\mathbf{A}^m = \mathbf{0}\) for all \(m > L\). Consequently, \((\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})\) is always invertible over \(\mathbb{R}\), and Boolean transitive closure terminates in at most \(L\) matrix multiplications:

    \[\mathcal{R}_{\text{bool}} = \bigvee_{m=0}^L \mathbb{I}(\mathbf{A}^m > 0), \qquad (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1} = \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}^2 + \dots + \mathbf{A}^L\]

    Worked Numerical Example (5-Node ML Chain): Suppose nodes are ordered \([e_1, a_1, e_2, a_2, e_3]\) along the causal chain \(e_1 \to a_1 \to e_2 \to a_2 \to e_3\) (\(L = 4\)). The strict upper-triangular adjacency matrix has \(1\)s on the first superdiagonal (\(\mathbf{A}_{12}=\mathbf{A}_{23}=\mathbf{A}_{34}=\mathbf{A}_{45}=1\)). Computing matrix powers produces 2-hop links in \(\mathbf{A}^2\) (\(\mathbf{A}^2_{13}=\mathbf{A}^2_{24}=\mathbf{A}^2_{35}=1\)), 3-hop links in \(\mathbf{A}^3\), and root-to-checkpoint reachability \(\mathbf{A}^4_{15}=1\), with \(\mathbf{A}^5 = \mathbf{0}\). Summing \(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^2+\mathbf{A}^3+\mathbf{A}^4\) yields a full upper-triangular reachability matrix \(\mathcal{R}\), confirming column 5 (\(\text{Ancestry}(e_3) = \{e_1, a_1, e_2, a_2, e_3\}\)). If a manual edit severs \(e_1 \to a_1\) (making \(\mathbf{A}_{12} = 0\)), row 1 drops to [1, 0, 0, 0, 0], proving mathematically that \(e_3\) can no longer be traced to raw source \(e_1\).

    Definition 8.3.3 (Codebook Categorical Mapping Fidelity Score)

    Let a discrete or encoded survey attribute \(X\) comprise \(N\) empirical observations \(\{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N\}\), and let \(\mathcal{M}: \text{Dom}(X) \to \mathcal{S}\) denote the versioned codebook dictionary mapping raw numeric/symbolic tokens into verified semantic labels \(\mathcal{S}\) (explicitly distinguishing valid domain codes from reserved missingness sentinels such as 77 = Refused or 99 = Don't Know).

    The Codebook Fidelity Score \(\mathcal{F}_{\text{codebook}} \in [0, 1]\) is defined as:

    \[\mathcal{F}_{\text{codebook}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}\left( x_i \in \text{Dom}(\mathcal{M}) \;\land\; \mathcal{M}(x_i) \ne \text{UNKNOWN} \right)\]

    Worked Calculation: Given raw survey batch \(X = [1, 2, 1, 99, 2, 42]\) (\(N = 6\)) and codebook \(\mathcal{M} = \{1 \mapsto \text{Positive}, 2 \mapsto \text{Negative}, 99 \mapsto \text{Missing\_Refused}\}\), five entries satisfy \(x_i \in \text{Dom}(\mathcal{M})\) (note that 99 is a valid codebook entry because its non-response semantic is explicitly captured), whereas 42 triggers an unmapped outlier error (\(\text{UNKNOWN}\)). Thus \(\mathcal{F}_{\text{codebook}} = \frac{5}{6} \approx 0.8333\) (\(83.33\%\)). CI ingestion gates typically require \(\mathcal{F}_{\text{codebook}} \ge 0.95\) (or strictly \(1.00\) in regulated clinical trials) before data promotion.

    Definition 8.3.4 (Lineage Topological & Cyclomatic Complexity)

    The structural maintainability and cascade risk of a data engineering pipeline is governed by its Lineage Cyclomatic Complexity \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{lineage}}\):

    \[\mathcal{M}_{\text{lineage}} = |\mathcal{E}| - |\mathcal{V}| + 2P\]

    where \(|\mathcal{E}|\) is the number of active dependency edges, \(|\mathcal{V}|\) is the total vertex count, and \(P\) is the number of weakly connected components in \(\mathcal{G}\). Notice that for a clean single-pipeline chain plus one unlinked legacy dump (\(|\mathcal{V}| = 6, |\mathcal{E}| = 4, P = 2\)), \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{lineage}} = 4 - 6 + 2(2) = 2\). Conversely, a high-branching tangled workflow (\(\mathcal{M}_{\text{lineage}} > 15\)) signals multi-hop coupling prone to silent data cascades (Sambasivan et al., 2021), while severing upstream links fragments components (\(P \uparrow\)) and breaks downstream auditability.

    Definition 8.3.5 (SemVer 2.0.0 Dominance Lattice & Hash-Chained Releases)

    When publishing a dataset release \(D_{t+1}\) relative to baseline \(D_t\) with schema \(S_t\), semantic version transitions follow a strict total dominance lattice \(\text{MAJOR} \succ \text{MINOR} \succ \text{PATCH}\) (Pacheco et al., 2024; Preston-Werner, 2013):

    \[\Delta \text{SemVer}(D_{t+1}, D_t) = \begin{cases} \text{MAJOR} & \text{if } \text{BreakingSchemaChange}(S_{t+1}, S_t) = 1 \\ \text{MINOR} & \text{else if } \text{AdditiveSchemaExtension}(S_{t+1}, S_t) = 1 \;\lor\; \frac{|\Delta N|}{N_t} > \tau_{\text{volume}} \\ \text{PATCH} & \text{else if } \text{CodebookOrLabelFixOnly}(D_{t+1}, D_t) = 1 \\ \text{NOOP} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

    To guarantee tamper-evident history across releases, each published manifest is cryptographically linked to its immediate predecessor hash \(H_{t-1}\) in a Merkle commit chain:

    \[H_t = \mathcal{H}_{\text{SHA-256}}\left( \text{Manifest}_t \,\|\, \text{SemVer}_t \,\|\, H_{t-1} \right)\]

    Any retroactive alteration to raw counts, codebook definitions, or lineage hashes invalidates \(H_t\) and all subsequent downstream checkpoints.

    💻 Runnable Executable Python Implementation

    The following production script implements:

    1. Pydantic V2 schema models for provenance nodes, lineage audit reports, codebooks, and hash-chained SemVer release manifests.
    2. W3C PROV-O nilpotent transitive reachability matrix via adjacency matrix powers.
    3. Codebook categorical mapping and sentinel-aware fidelity scoring.
    4. Deterministic SemVer 2.0.0 dominance lattice and SHA-256 Merkle parent-hash chaining.
    """
Lesson 8.3 Verification Suite: Codebooks, W3C PROV-O Lineage & SemVer Release Engine
Verified against Moreau et al. (2011), Herschel et al. (2017), Preston-Werner (2013).
"""

import hashlib
from typing import Dict, List, Tuple
import numpy as np
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field


class ProvenanceNode(BaseModel):
    id: str
    kind: str = Field(..., description="'entity', 'activity', 'agent'")
    label: str


class LineageGraphReport(BaseModel):
    total_vertices: int
    total_edges: int
    connected_components: int
    cyclomatic_complexity: int
    model_depends_on_raw: bool
    unrelated_reachable: bool


class CodebookReport(BaseModel):
    total_records: int
    valid_codes: int
    fidelity_score: float
    mapped_sample: List[str]


class ReleaseVersionReport(BaseModel):
    base_version: str
    breaking_version: str
    minor_version: str
    patch_version: str
    dominance_override_version: str
    chained_commit_hash: str


class ProvenanceAndReleaseManager:
    def __init__(self):
        self.nodes: List[ProvenanceNode] = []
        self.node_to_idx: Dict[str, int] = {}
        self.edges: List[Tuple[int, int]] = []

    def add_node(self, node: ProvenanceNode):
        if node.id not in self.node_to_idx:
            self.node_to_idx[node.id] = len(self.nodes)
            self.nodes.append(node)

    def add_edge(self, source_id: str, target_id: str):
        """Directed causal edge: source -> target."""
        s = self.node_to_idx[source_id]
        t = self.node_to_idx[target_id]
        self.edges.append((s, t))

    def compute_reachability(self) -> np.ndarray:
        n = len(self.nodes)
        adj = np.zeros((n, n), dtype=int)
        for u, v in self.edges:
            adj[u, v] = 1

        reachability = np.eye(n, dtype=int)
        power = adj.copy()
        for _ in range(n):
            if not np.any(power):
                break
            reachability = np.bitwise_or(reachability, (power > 0).astype(int))
            power = np.dot(power, adj)
        return reachability

    def compute_cyclomatic_complexity(self, connected_components: int = 1) -> int:
        return len(self.edges) - len(self.nodes) + 2 * connected_components

    @staticmethod
    def apply_codebook(raw_values: List[int], codebook: Dict[int, str]) -> Tuple[List[str], int, float]:
        mapped: List[str] = []
        valid_count = 0
        for val in raw_values:
            if val in codebook and codebook[val] != "UNKNOWN":
                mapped.append(codebook[val])
                valid_count += 1
            else:
                mapped.append("UNKNOWN")
        fidelity = valid_count / len(raw_values)
        return mapped, valid_count, round(fidelity, 4)

    @staticmethod
    def determine_semver_increment(
        current_version: str,
        schema_removed_cols: bool,
        schema_added_cols: bool,
        label_fixes_only: bool,
    ) -> str:
        """Applies strict SemVer 2.0.0 dominance lattice: MAJOR > MINOR > PATCH."""
        major, minor, patch = map(int, current_version.split("."))
        if schema_removed_cols:
            return f"{major + 1}.0.0"
        if schema_added_cols:
            return f"{major}.{minor + 1}.0"
        if label_fixes_only:
            return f"{major}.{minor}.{patch + 1}"
        return current_version

    @staticmethod
    def compute_release_commit_hash(manifest_summary: str, semver: str, parent_hash: str) -> str:
        payload = f"{manifest_summary}|{semver}|{parent_hash}".encode("utf-8")
        return hashlib.sha256(payload).hexdigest()


# =====================================================================
# Verification & Execution Benchmark
# =====================================================================
if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("=" * 65)
    print("CODEBOOK, PROVENANCE & SEMVER RELEASE VERIFICATION")
    print("=" * 65)

    manager = ProvenanceAndReleaseManager()

    # 1. Codebook Mapping & Fidelity Test
    codebook = {1: "Positive", 2: "Negative", 99: "Missing_Not_Reported"}
    raw_survey = [1, 2, 1, 99, 2, 42]  # Sentinel 99 is documented; 42 is unmapped
    labels, valid_cnt, fidelity = manager.apply_codebook(raw_survey, codebook)

    cb_report = CodebookReport(
        total_records=len(raw_survey),
        valid_codes=valid_cnt,
        fidelity_score=fidelity,
        mapped_sample=labels,
    )
    print(f"[AUDIT] Mapped Categories: {cb_report.mapped_sample}")
    print(f"[AUDIT] Codebook Fidelity: {cb_report.fidelity_score * 100:.2f}% ({valid_cnt}/{len(raw_survey)} Valid)")
    assert cb_report.fidelity_score == 0.8333, "Expected 83.33% codebook fidelity"

    # 2. W3C PROV-O Transitive Reachability Test (6-node DAG matching Simulator 8.3)
    nodes = [
        ProvenanceNode(id="raw_sensor_e1", kind="entity", label="Raw Telemetry"),
        ProvenanceNode(id="normalize_a1", kind="activity", label="Normalization ETL"),
        ProvenanceNode(id="cleaned_split_e2", kind="entity", label="Cleaned Parquet"),
        ProvenanceNode(id="train_sgd_a2", kind="activity", label="SGD Training"),
        ProvenanceNode(id="model_weights_e3", kind="entity", label="Model Checkpoint"),
        ProvenanceNode(id="unrelated_e4", kind="entity", label="Unrelated Legacy Dump"),
    ]
    for n in nodes:
        manager.add_node(n)

    manager.add_edge("raw_sensor_e1", "normalize_a1")
    manager.add_edge("normalize_a1", "cleaned_split_e2")
    manager.add_edge("cleaned_split_e2", "train_sgd_a2")
    manager.add_edge("train_sgd_a2", "model_weights_e3")

    reach = manager.compute_reachability()

    🏢 Real-World Industrial Case Studies

    Case Study 1: CDC National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) Top-Coding & Codebooks

    The US Centers for Disease Control (CDC) conducts NHANES, clinically examining over \(5{,}000\) participants annually across thousands of dietary, biomarker, and socioeconomic columns.

    NHANES Top-Coding & Sentinel Missingness Discipline

    • The Top-Coding Pitfall: In the 2007–2008 survey cycle, variable RIDAGEYR recorded age in years but top-coded all values $\ge 80$ as 80 to protect participant anonymity for nonagenarians and centenarians. Naive machine learning engineers treating 80 as an exact continuous measurement systematically skewed extreme-elderly cardiovascular risk predictors.
    • Missingness Sentinels: Survey responses distinguish refusal and non-knowledge via explicit numeric codes: 7 / 77 = "Refused", 9 / 99 = "Don't Know", and . = "Skip-Pattern Inapplicable". Passing 99 unmapped into gradient-boosted trees or regressions injects catastrophic artificial outliers.
    • The Architectural Remedy: The CDC distributes Formal SAS Codebooks paired with Machine-Readable XML Dictionaries specifying exact censoring thresholds, skip logic, and denominator scopes, preserving epidemiological validity across thousands of public health studies (Michener et al., 1997).

    Case Study 2: The Duke University Oncology Scandal & Forensic Provenance Reconstruction

    Between 2006 and 2010, genomic signatures published from Duke University purported to match chemotherapy regimens to individual cancer patients using Affymetrix microarray profiles, launching three clinical trials (Baggerly & Coombes, 2009).

    Forensic Bioinformatics Failure: Severed Provenance Chain

    • The Undocumented Manual Edit: Raw microarray CEL files passed through unversioned Excel spreadsheets instead of automated W3C PROV-O pipelines. Manual cut-and-paste operations introduced off-by-one column index shifts and swapped drug "Sensitive" vs. "Resistant" label blocks.
    • Forensic Cost: Because intermediate artifacts lacked cryptographic hashes and execution provenance ($a_1: \text{Manual Excel}$ severed $e_1 \to a_1$), external biostatisticians required over $1{,}500$ hours of reverse-engineering to reconstruct the exact row shifts that invalidated published predictors (Baggerly & Coombes, 2009).
    • Clinical & Institutional Consequence: All three patient trials were suspended, over two dozen oncology papers were retracted, and biomedical journals subsequently mandated machine-verifiable codebooks, raw-to-result scripts, and end-to-end lineage manifests prior to peer-review acceptance.

    Self-Assessment 1: W3C PROV-O Entity-Activity-Agent Triad Mapping

    In an autonomous driving training pipeline, raw LiDAR point cloud sweeps (lidar_raw.bag) are filtered for atmospheric rain noise by an open-source C++ cleaning binary (voxel_filter) scheduled automatically by an Apache Airflow worker instance (airflow-worker-prod-04). Map these three components into the formal W3C PROV-O ontology.

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    1. Entity (prov:Entity): lidar_raw.bag (the source entity) and lidar_filtered.parquet (the generated downstream entity). 2. Activity (prov:Activity): The voxel filtering process execution (used(lidar_raw.bag) and wasGeneratedBy(voxel_filter)). 3. Agent (prov:Agent): airflow-worker-prod-04 (the automated executor associated via wasAssociatedWith).

    Self-Assessment 2: Cryptographic Hash Chaining & Tamper-Evident Lineage Audits

    How does linking dataset version metadata with SHA-256 parent hash pointers guarantee tamper-evident data provenance across distributed storage clusters?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: In a Merkle DAG hash chain, every commit hash \(H_t\) is computed deterministically over its immediate dataset manifest and its parent commit hash pointer:

    \[H_t = \mathcal{H}_{\text{SHA-256}}\left( \text{Manifest}_t \,\|\, H_{t-1} \right)\]

    If an unauthorized actor retroactively modifies a single feature value or label in historical dataset \(D_0\), its hash changes to \(H_0'\). This perturbation cascades through all subsequent child hashes:

    \[H_1' = \mathcal{H}(M_1 \,\|\, H_0'), \quad H_2' = \mathcal{H}(M_2 \,\|\, H_1'), \quad \dots\]

    This immediately breaks the cryptographic signature of the latest production commit, exposing the exact historical tampering point during automated CI/CD audits.

    Self-Assessment 3: Transitive Reachability Matrix Powers in Forensic Anomaly Tracing

    Suppose a machine learning model checkpoint \(e_{\text{model}}\) exhibits unexpected bias in production. The pipeline contains 12 transformation activities. Why does evaluating the transitive reachability matrix \(\mathcal{R} = \sum_{m=0}^L \mathbf{A}^m\) guarantee identification of all upstream raw sources without risking infinite circular recursion?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: Because data pipeline workflows are strictly acyclic Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs), no computational step can depend on its own future output.

    The adjacency matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) of any DAG with topological depth \(L\) is strictly nilpotent:

    \[\mathbf{A}^m = \mathbf{0}, \quad \forall m > L\]

    Consequently, the power series terminates after exactly \(L\) matrix products. The reachability vector \(\mathcal{R}_{*, \text{model}}\) provides a mathematically complete binary mask of every upstream entity and transformation activity that contributed to the model checkpoint.

    Self-Assessment 4: Codebook Missingness Encoding vs. Unchecked Numerical Imputation

    In the CDC NHANES survey, why is replacing survey missingness codes (such as 77 = Refused, 99 = Don't Know) with default numerical zero or mean imputation dangerous for training neural network classifiers?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Answer: In survey methodology, non-response is rarely Missing Completely at Random (MCAR). A participant refusing to answer an income or substance abuse question (99) carries strong behavioral and demographic information.

    Treating 99 as a continuous numeric value introduces a severe artificial outlier that distorts linear regression gradients and distance metrics. Conversely, imputing the mean or zero falsely asserts that the participant had average or zero behavior.

    A formal codebook maps 99 to an explicit categorical missingness token (Missing_Refused), allowing tree-based or embedding layers to learn missingness mechanisms explicitly without numerical corruption.
