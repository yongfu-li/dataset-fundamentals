    No human annotator—regardless of domain expertise—produces perfectly noise-free annotations. In clinical diagnostics, autonomous driving perception, and legal document analysis, subjective interpretation, linguistic ambiguity, edge cases, and human fatigue inevitably cause annotators to disagree [Cohen1960, Dawid1979, Gulshan2016].

    Quality control in dataset engineering relies on statistical reliability theory: measuring inter-annotator agreement (IAA) beyond chance and employing probabilistic latent-variable models to estimate true unobserved labels while characterizing individual worker confusion matrices [Fleiss1971, Krippendorff1980, Artstein2008].

    1.1 The Failure of Raw Percent Agreement

    Given two annotators rating \(N\) instances into \(K\) categorical classes, the observed proportion of raw agreement is:

    \[ P_o = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(y_{i,1} = y_{i,2}) \]

    Raw agreement \(P_o\) is fundamentally deceptive when class distributions are imbalanced.

    [!WARNING] The Feinstein-Cicchetti Prevalence Paradox If a rare pathology occurs in only \(1\%\) of medical scans, two radiologists who blindly label every single scan as "Negative" achieve \(P_o = 99.0\%\), despite possessing zero diagnostic discriminatory power [Feinstein1990]. Chance-corrected agreement metrics are mathematically mandatory.

    1.2 Cohen's Kappa (\(\kappa\))

    Cohen (1960) [Cohen1960] isolated true consensus from chance by subtracting the expected hypothetical agreement \(P_e\) under independent marginal label distributions:

    \[ \kappa = \frac{P_o - P_e}{1 - P_e} \]

    Where \(P_e\) is computed from the marginal distributions of Rater 1 (\(p_{1,k}\)) and Rater 2 (\(p_{2,k}\)):

    \[ P_e = \sum_{k=1}^K p_{1,k} \cdot p_{2,k} \]
    • \(\kappa = 1.0\): Perfect deterministic consensus.
    • \(\kappa = 0.0\): Agreement exactly equal to random guessing.
    • \(\kappa < 0.0\): Systematic disagreement (inverse association).
    • Landis & Koch Benchmark Ratings [Landis1977]:
    • \(\kappa < 0.40\): Poor / Unacceptable reliability.
    • \(0.40 \le \kappa < 0.60\): Moderate agreement (guidelines need tightening).
    • \(0.60 \le \kappa < 0.75\): Substantial agreement.
    • \(\kappa \ge 0.75\): Excellent / Publishable scientific quality.

    1.3 Weighted Kappa for Ordinal Distances (\(\kappa_w\))

    When classes have natural ordering (e.g., Likert survey scales \(1 \dots 5\), cancer stages \(\text{I} \dots \text{IV}\)), confusing Stage I with Stage II is far less egregious than confusing Stage I with Stage IV [Cohen1968].

    Weighted Kappa applies a distance penalty matrix \(\mathbf{W} \in [0, 1]^{K \times K}\):

    \[ \kappa_w = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^K \sum_{j=1}^K w_{i,j} \cdot O_{i,j}}{\sum_{i=1}^K \sum_{j=1}^K w_{i,j} \cdot E_{i,j}} \]

    Where \(O_{i,j}\) and \(E_{i,j}\) denote the observed and expected proportions in cell \((i, j)\). Standard weighting functions include: - Linear Distance: \(w_{i,j} = \frac{|i - j|}{K - 1}\) - Quadratic Distance (Fleiss-Cohen standard): \(w_{i,j} = \frac{(i - j)^2}{(K - 1)^2}\)

    1.4 Fleiss' Kappa (\(\kappa_{\text{Fleiss}}\)): Arbitrary Rater Pools

    When \(m\) raters independently rate \(N\) items into \(K\) categories (where the identity of the \(m\) raters may vary per item, as in crowdsourcing), Fleiss' Kappa [Fleiss1971] defines the agreement on item \(i\) as:

    \[ P_i = \frac{1}{m(m - 1)} \sum_{j=1}^K n_{i,j}(n_{i,j} - 1) = \frac{1}{m(m - 1)} \left( \sum_{j=1}^K n_{i,j}^2 - m \right) \]

    Where \(n_{i,j}\) is the number of raters who assigned item \(i\) to category \(j\). The mean observed agreement \(\bar{P}\) and chance agreement \(\bar{P}_e\) across all \(N\) items are:

    \[ \bar{P} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N P_i, \quad p_j = \frac{1}{N \cdot m} \sum_{i=1}^N n_{i,j}, \quad \bar{P}_e = \sum_{j=1}^K p_j^2 \]
    \[ \kappa_{\text{Fleiss}} = \frac{\bar{P} - \bar{P}_e}{1 - \bar{P}_e} \]

    1.5 Krippendorff's Alpha (\(\alpha\))

    Krippendorff's Alpha [Krippendorff1980] generalizes reliability coefficients across incomplete designs (where annotators rate arbitrary overlapping subsets) and supports nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio difference metrics \(\delta^2(v, v')\):

    \[ \alpha = 1 - \frac{D_o}{D_e} = 1 - \frac{(n - 1) \sum_{v} \sum_{v'} o_{v, v'} \cdot \delta^2(v, v')}{\sum_{v} \sum_{v'} n_v \cdot n_{v'} \cdot \delta^2(v, v')} \]

    Where \(D_o\) is the observed disagreement, \(D_e\) is the expected chance disagreement, \(o_{v, v'}\) is the coincidence matrix count, and \(n_v\) is the marginal category frequency.

    2. The Dawid-Skene Generative Model

    When aggregating multi-annotator datasets, naive majority voting treats all annotators as equally competent. If an expert is outvoted by two incompetent crowdsourced workers or spammers, majority voting adopts the incorrect label [Dawid1979].

    The Dawid-Skene Model applies the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm to simultaneously infer: 1. The true latent class distribution \(T_{i, j} = P(Y_i^* = j \mid \mathbf{y}_i)\) for instance \(i\). 2. The individual confusion matrix \(\pi_{j, l}^{(m)} = P(Y_i^{(m)} = l \mid Y_i^* = j)\) for each worker \(m\). 3. The class prevalence prior \(p_j = P(Y^* = j)\).

    2.1 E-Step: Posterior Class Probability

    Given current parameter estimates \((\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(m)}, \mathbf{p})\), compute the posterior probability that instance \(i\) belongs to true latent class \(j\):

    \[ T_{i, j}^{(t)} = \frac{p_j^{(t)} \prod_{m=1}^M \prod_{l=1}^K \left( \pi_{j, l}^{(m)(t)} \right)^{\mathbb{I}(y_i^{(m)} = l)}}{\sum_{c=1}^K p_c^{(t)} \prod_{m=1}^M \prod_{l=1}^K \left( \pi_{c, l}^{(m)(t)} \right)^{\mathbb{I}(y_i^{(m)} = l)}} \]

    2.2 M-Step: Parameter Maximization

    Update the confusion matrices \(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(m)}\) and class priors \(\mathbf{p}\) using the soft assignment probabilities \(T_{i, j}^{(t)}\):

    \[ \pi_{j, l}^{(m)(t+1)} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N T_{i, j}^{(t)} \cdot \mathbb{I}(y_i^{(m)} = l)}{\sum_{i=1}^N T_{i, j}^{(t)}}, \quad p_j^{(t+1)} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N T_{i, j}^{(t)} \]

    3. Landmark Empirical Case Studies

    Benchmark / Study Domain Quality Challenge Solution & Impact
    Diabetic Retinopathy Screening [Gulshan2016, Krause2018] Medical Imaging (Fundus Photography) Unweighted Cohen's \(\kappa\) between certified ophthalmologists was only \(0.55\text{–}0.65\) on 5-class severity, threatening clinical validation. Applied Quadratic Weighted Kappa (\(\kappa_w = 0.84\)) and convened 3-retina specialist adjudication panels for discordant cases, enabling FDA-grade model validation.
    CheXpert Chest Radiographs [Irvin2019] Clinical Radiology Reports (224k scans) Radiologists frequently documented equivocal observations ("cannot rule out pneumonia"), creating massive inter-rater variance. Developed uncertainty label mapping policies (Uncertain-as-Positive vs. Uncertain-as-Negative vs. Multi-Expert Dawid-Skene), improving downstream CheXNet AUC by \(+0.04\).
    SNLI & MultiNLI [Bowman2015, Williams2018] NLP Natural Language Inference Crowdsourced annotators exhibited persistent 3–2 splits on subjective sentence entailment pairs. Proved that human label variance stems from valid linguistic ambiguity rather than worker negligence; modern benchmarks now preserve soft multi-annotator probability distributions rather than forcing brittle majority-vote labels.
    ImageNet Multilabel Re-Assessment [Beyer2020] Vision Classification Real-world images frequently contain multiple valid objects, but ImageNet enforced single-label ground truth. Extensive multi-annotator reassessment revealed that top models were being falsely penalized for detecting unannotated secondary objects; multi-rater consensus boosted true model accuracy by \(\approx 10\%\).

    5. Production Python Implementation: Cohen's Kappa, Fleiss' Kappa & Dawid-Skene EM

    The production Python engine below enforces schema validation for multi-rater contingency tables, calculates chance-corrected unweighted and quadratic weighted agreement, and executes the complete Dawid-Skene Expectation-Maximization consensus engine.

    """
inter_annotator_agreement_engine.py
Production-grade Quality Control & Reliability Engine for Multi-Annotator Datasets.
Computes Cohen's Kappa, Quadratic Weighted Kappa, Fleiss' Generalized Multi-Rater Kappa,
and the Dawid-Skene Latent Truth Expectation-Maximization Estimator.
"""

from typing import List, Tuple, Dict, Any, Optional
import numpy as np
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, model_validator


# =====================================================================
# Pydantic V2 Schemas for Multi-Rater Reliability
# =====================================================================

class ContingencyMatrix2x2(BaseModel):
    """Represents a 2x2 contingency matrix between two independent raters."""
    n00: int = Field(..., ge=0, description="Both raters voted Class 0")
    n01: int = Field(..., ge=0, description="Rater 1 voted 0, Rater 2 voted 1")
    n10: int = Field(..., ge=0, description="Rater 1 voted 1, Rater 2 voted 0")
    n11: int = Field(..., ge=0, description="Both raters voted Class 1")

    @property
    def total_items(self) -> int:
        return self.n00 + self.n01 + self.n10 + self.n11

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def validate_non_empty(self) -> "ContingencyMatrix2x2":
        if self.total_items == 0:
            raise ValueError("Contingency matrix cannot have zero total observations.")
        return self


class MultiRaterRatingsMatrix(BaseModel):
    """Represents N items rated by m raters across K categories."""
    matrix: List[List[int]] = Field(..., description="Shape [N, K], cell (i, j) = vote count")
    num_raters_per_item: int = Field(..., ge=2, description="Fixed number of raters m per item")

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def validate_row_sums(self) -> "MultiRaterRatingsMatrix":
        for i, row in enumerate(self.matrix):
            if sum(row) != self.num_raters_per_item:
                raise ValueError(
                    f"Row {i} has {sum(row)} votes, expected exactly m={self.num_raters_per_item}"
                )
        return self


# =====================================================================
# Inter-Annotator Agreement Computation Engine
# =====================================================================

class InterAnnotatorAgreementEngine:
    @classmethod
    def compute_cohen_kappa(cls, cm: ContingencyMatrix2x2) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
        """
        Computes Cohen's Kappa for 2x2 contingency matrix.
        Returns: (kappa, observed_agreement P_o, expected_chance P_e)
        """
        N = cm.total_items
        po = (cm.n00 + cm.n11) / N

        # Marginal probabilities
        r1_c0 = (cm.n00 + cm.n01) / N
        r1_c1 = (cm.n10 + cm.n11) / N

        r2_c0 = (cm.n00 + cm.n10) / N
        r2_c1 = (cm.n01 + cm.n11) / N

        pe = (r1_c0 * r2_c0) + (r1_c1 * r2_c1)

        if abs(1.0 - pe) < 1e-12:
            kappa = 1.0
        else:
            kappa = (po - pe) / (1.0 - pe)

        return round(float(kappa), 4), round(float(po), 4), round(float(pe), 4)

    @classmethod
    def compute_quadratic_weighted_kappa(
        cls,
        r1: np.ndarray,
        r2: np.ndarray,
        num_classes: int
    ) -> float:
        """Computes Quadratic Weighted Kappa for ordinal scale ratings."""
        N = len(r1)
        if N == 0:
            return 0.0

        # Construct observed confusion matrix O
        O = np.zeros((num_classes, num_classes))
        for a, b in zip(r1, r2):
            O[a, b] += 1
        O /= N

        # Construct expected matrix E under marginal independence
        hist1 = np.bincount(r1, minlength=num_classes) / N
        hist2 = np.bincount(r2, minlength=num_classes) / N
        E = np.outer(hist1, hist2)

        # Quadratic weights W_ij = (i - j)^2 / (K - 1)^2
        W = np.zeros((num_classes, num_classes))
        denom = float((num_classes - 1) ** 2) if num_classes > 1 else 1.0
        for i in range(num_classes):
            for j in range(num_classes):
                W[i, j] = ((i - j) ** 2) / denom

        num = np.sum(W * O)
        den = np.sum(W * E)

        if abs(den) < 1e-12:
            return 1.0
        return round(float(1.0 - (num / den)), 4)

    @classmethod
    def compute_fleiss_kappa(cls, ratings: MultiRaterRatingsMatrix) -> float:
        """Computes Fleiss' Multi-Rater Kappa across N items and K classes."""
        mat = np.array(ratings.matrix)
        N, K = mat.shape
        m = ratings.num_raters_per_item

        # Compute item agreement P_i
        sum_sq = np.sum(mat ** 2, axis=1)
        p_i = (sum_sq - m) / (m * (m - 1))
        p_bar = float(np.mean(p_i))

        # Marginal category probabilities
        p_j = np.sum(mat, axis=0) / (N * m)
        pe_bar = float(np.sum(p_j ** 2))

        if abs(1.0 - pe_bar) < 1e-12:
            return 1.0
        return round(float((p_bar - pe_bar) / (1.0 - pe_bar)), 4)

    @classmethod
    def dawid_skene_em(
        cls,
        annotations: Dict[int, Dict[int, int]],
        num_classes: int,
        max_iter: int = 50,
        tol: float = 1e-5
    ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, Dict[int, np.ndarray], np.ndarray]:
        """
        Executes Dawid-Skene Expectation-Maximization latent consensus inference.
        Returns: (T_posteriors [N, K], worker_confusion_matrices {worker_id: [K, K]}, class_priors [K])
        """
        items = sorted(annotations.keys())
        annotators = sorted({w for itm in annotations.values() for w in itm.keys()})
        N = len(items)

        # Initialize posteriors via majority vote
        T = np.zeros((N, num_classes))
        for idx, item in enumerate(items):
            votes = list(annotations[item].values())
            counts = np.bincount(votes, minlength=num_classes)
            T[idx] = counts / np.sum(counts)

        priors = np.mean(T, axis=0)
        pi: Dict[int, np.ndarray] = {}

        for _ in range(max_iter):
            # M-Step: Compute confusion matrix for each annotator
            pi = {w: np.zeros((num_classes, num_classes)) for w in annotators}
            for w in annotators:
                denom = np.zeros(num_classes)
                for idx, item in enumerate(items):
                    if w in annotations[item]:
                        obs = annotations[item][w]
                        for j in range(num_classes):
                            pi[w][j, obs] += T[idx, j]
                            denom[j] += T[idx, j]
                for j in range(num_classes):
                    if denom[j] > 0:
                        pi[w][j] /= denom[j]
                    else:
                        pi[w][j] = 1.0 / num_classes

            # E-Step: Update posterior T_ij
            new_T = np.zeros((N, num_classes))
            for idx, item in enumerate(items):
                for j in range(num_classes):
                    prod = priors[j]
                    for w, obs in annotations[item].items():
                        prod *= pi[w][j, obs]
                    new_T[idx, j] = prod
                row_sum = np.sum(new_T[idx])
                if row_sum > 0:
                    new_T[idx] /= row_sum
                else:
                    new_T[idx] = 1.0 / num_classes

            diff = np.max(np.abs(new_T - T))
            T = new_T
            priors = np.mean(T, axis=0)

            if diff < tol:
                break

        return np.round(T, 4), {w: np.round(pi[w], 4) for w in pi}, np.round(priors, 4)

    6. Diagnostic Self-Assessment Scenarios

    Scenario 1: The Feinstein-Cicchetti Prevalence Paradox in Mammography Screening

    Two senior radiologists review 10,000 screening mammograms for micro-calcifications indicating early-stage malignancy. The underlying population prevalence is \(0.5\%\) (\(50\) positive scans). Both radiologists independently classify \(9,950\) scans as Negative and \(50\) scans as Positive. They agree on \(9,900\) Negative scans and \(20\) Positive scans (disagreeing on \(80\) scans total). 1. Compute their raw observed agreement \(P_o\). 2. Compute Cohen's Kappa \(\kappa\). 3. Explain why \(\kappa\) appears low despite raw agreement exceeding \(99\%\).

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    1. Quantitative Calculation: - Total instances \(N = 10{,}000\). - Observed agreement: \(P_o = \frac{9{,}900 + 20}{10{,}000} = \frac{9{,}920}{10{,}000} = 0.992 \quad (99.2\%)\). - Marginal probabilities: $\(p_{1,\text{neg}} = \frac{9{,}900 + 40}{10{,}000} = 0.994, \quad p_{1,\text{pos}} = 0.006\)$ $\(p_{2,\text{neg}} = \frac{9{,}900 + 40}{10{,}000} = 0.994, \quad p_{2,\text{pos}} = 0.006\)$ - Expected chance agreement: $\(P_e = (0.994 \times 0.994) + (0.006 \times 0.006) = 0.988036 + 0.000036 = 0.988072\)$ - Cohen's Kappa: $\(\kappa = \frac{P_o - P_e}{1 - P_e} = \frac{0.992000 - 0.988072}{1 - 0.988072} = \frac{0.003928}{0.011928} \approx 0.329 \quad (32.9\%)\)$

    2. Clinical Interpretation (Prevalence Paradox): - While raw agreement is an extraordinary \(99.2\%\), Cohen's Kappa falls into the Poor Reliability (< 0.40) tier. - Cause: Because \(99.4\%\) of the cohort is negative, two raters with zero training would agree on \(98.8\%\) of scans purely by chance. Of the tiny window where discriminatory skill matters (\(1 - P_e = 1.19\%\)), the radiologists disagreed on \(80\) out of \(100\) discordant/borderline opportunities. - Engineering Correction: Evaluate positive and negative specific agreement indices (\(P_{\text{pos}} = \frac{2 \times 20}{20 + 20 + 40 + 40} = 0.333\)) and sample balanced enrichment cohorts for quality audits.

    Scenario 2: Unweighted vs. Quadratic Weighted Kappa on Likert Staging Scales

    A medical dataset classifies prostate cancer biopsy Gleason scores into 5 ordinal risk tiers (\(1 = \text{Low Risk}\) to \(5 = \text{Metastatic}\)). In an audit of 100 cases, two pathologists agree identically on 70 cases. On the remaining 30 cases, Pathologist A rates the case as Tier 2 while Pathologist B rates it as Tier 3 (an adjacent boundary disagreement). 1. Contrast how unweighted Cohen's \(\kappa\) and Quadratic Weighted Kappa \(\kappa_w\) evaluate this disagreement. 2. Which metric provides the correct engineering gate for training an ordinal regression loss function?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    1. Penalty Comparison: - Unweighted Cohen's \(\kappa\): Treats every categorical disagreement as a binary indicator \(\mathbb{I}(y_1 \ne y_2) = 1\). A minor discrepancy between adjacent Tier 2 and Tier 3 is penalized identically to a catastrophic divergence between Tier 1 (Benign) and Tier 5 (Metastatic). Consequently, unweighted \(\kappa\) drops steeply to \(\approx 0.55\text{–}0.60\) (Moderate). - Quadratic Weighted \(\kappa_w\): Formulates weight penalty \(w_{i,j} = \frac{(i - j)^2}{(K - 1)^2}\). For adjacent classes (\(|i - j| = 1\)) on a 5-class scale (\(K=5\)): $\(w_{2,3} = \frac{(2 - 3)^2}{(5 - 1)^2} = \frac{1}{16} = 0.0625\)$ In contrast, a severe divergence (\(|1 - 5| = 4\)) incurs penalty \(w_{1,5} = \frac{16}{16} = 1.00\). The adjacent discrepancies receive only \(6.25\%\) of the maximum penalty, yielding \(\kappa_w \approx 0.92\) (Excellent Consensus).

    2. Engineering Recommendation: Quadratic Weighted Kappa is the mathematically correct metric. Ordinal regression networks optimize smooth distance losses (e.g., Earth Mover's Distance, Mean Squared Error, or Coral Ordinal Loss). Penalizing adjacent human boundary disagreement as complete failure misaligns the validation gate with the physical loss geometry.

    Scenario 3: Dawid-Skene Latent Truth vs. Naive Majority Vote with Colluding Workers

    In a crowdsourced safety moderation queue, 5 annotators label text comments as Toxic (\(1\)) or Non-Toxic (\(0\)). Annotator 1 is a verified trusted senior moderator (\(\pi_{1,1}^{(1)} = 0.98, \pi_{0,0}^{(1)} = 0.99\)). Annotators 2, 3, 4, and 5 are low-effort click-farm workers who always submit \(y=0\) to maximize hourly speed. On an overtly toxic hate-speech comment, Annotator 1 marks \(y=1\) while Annotators 2–5 mark \(y=0\). 1. What is the consensus prediction under naive Majority Voting? 2. Explain how the Dawid-Skene EM model correctly recovers the true ground truth.

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    1. Naive Majority Vote: The votes are \(y^{(1)} = 1\), \(y^{(2)} = 0\), \(y^{(3)} = 0\), \(y^{(4)} = 0\), \(y^{(5)} = 0\). Votes for Class 0: \(4\). Votes for Class 1: \(1\). $\(\hat{Y}_{\text{majority}} = \arg\max(4, 1) = 0 \quad (\text{Non-Toxic})\)$ Majority voting adopts the fraudulent spammer label, allowing severe hate speech to corrupt the gold training set.

    2. Dawid-Skene EM Resolution: The Dawid-Skene model estimates each worker's confusion matrix \(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(m)}\) across all historical tasks. - For Worker 1: \(\pi_{1,1}^{(1)} \approx 0.98\) and \(\pi_{0,1}^{(1)} \approx 0.01\) (extremely high discriminative precision). - For Workers 2–5: \(\pi_{0,0}^{(m)} \approx 1.0\) and \(\pi_{1,0}^{(m)} \approx 1.0\) (Worker selects 0 regardless of truth; false negative rate \(\approx 100\%\)).

    In the E-step, posterior likelihoods are computed multiplicatively: $\(P(Y^* = 1 \mid \mathbf{y}) \propto p_1 \cdot \pi_{1,1}^{(1)} \cdot \prod_{m=2}^5 \pi_{1,0}^{(m)}\)$ $\(P(Y^* = 0 \mid \mathbf{y}) \propto p_0 \cdot \pi_{0,1}^{(1)} \cdot \prod_{m=2}^5 \pi_{0,0}^{(m)}\)$ Because Worker 1 almost never hallucinates a positive on clean text (\(\pi_{0,1}^{(1)} \approx 0.01\)), the likelihood for \(Y^*=0\) contains a near-zero multiplier (\(0.01\)). In contrast, Worker 1's true positive likelihood (\(\pi_{1,1}^{(1)} = 0.98\)) combined with the spammers' known tendency to vote 0 on positive instances (\(\pi_{1,0}^{(m)} \approx 1.0\)) dominates the posterior. Dawid-Skene infers \(P(Y^* = 1 \mid \mathbf{y}) > 0.95\), overriding the 4-to-1 majority vote.

    Scenario 4: Irreducible Linguistic Disagreement vs. Worker Incompetence in NLP

    In the Stanford Natural Language Inference (SNLI) benchmark, human annotators read the premise "A woman in a bridal gown is smiling while standing beside a cake." and the hypothesis "The woman is getting married." Across 5 annotators, 3 choose Entailment and 2 choose Neutral (arguing she could be a bridal model or bakery demonstrator). How should a machine learning engineering team handle this 3–2 split in a modern LLM fine-tuning pipeline?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Modern Dataset Engineering Approach (Distributional Ground Truth): 1. Do NOT Force Single-Label Pseudo-Truth: Collapsing the 3–2 vote into a hard "Entailment" majority label discards valid linguistic nuance and trains models to be overconfident on inherently ambiguous edge cases [Bowman2015, Beyer2020]. 2. Train on Soft Multi-Annotator Probabilities: Represent the ground truth as a categorical probability vector: $\(\mathbf{y}_{\text{gt}} = [P(\text{Entailment}) = 0.60, \; P(\text{Neutral}) = 0.40, \; P(\text{Contradiction}) = 0.00]\)$ Train LLMs and classifiers using Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence or Soft Cross-Entropy Loss: $\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{soft}} = - \sum_{k=1}^K y_{\text{gt}, k} \log \hat{y}_k\)$ 3. Adjudication for Guideline Drift: If the disagreement stems from conflicting guidelines (e.g., guidelines did not clarify whether common-sense plausibility constitutes entailment), update the annotator specification with concrete counterexamples.
