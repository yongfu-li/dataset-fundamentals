    Manual brute-force labeling of millions of records by human workers is economically unviable and operationally sluggish [Settles2009, Ratner2017]. In production machine learning, labeling is the dominant cost center and latency bottleneck. Modern data engineering deploys a spectrum of hybrid human-in-the-loop annotation techniques that systematically trade off human labor, heuristic programmatic rules, and model predictions.

    By strategically orchestrating Active Learning (sampling boundary instances), Weak Supervision (Snorkel generative label modeling), and Model-Assisted Pre-Labeling, organizations can reduce manual human labeling volume by \(70\% \text{ to } 90\%\) while preserving statistical generalization guarantees [Settles2009, Bach2019, Snow2008].

    1. The Spectrum of Annotation Paradigms & The Economic Frontier

    The fundamental annotation dilemma is governed by sample complexity under noisy or missing supervision [Angluin1988, Northcutt2021]. Practitioners must navigate five distinct paradigms along the cost-efficiency frontier:

    2. Mathematical Formalization of Active Learning

    In pool-based Active Learning [Settles2009], we maintain a small labeled training cohort \(\mathcal{L} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^M\) and a massive pool of unlabeled candidate records \(\mathcal{U} = \{x_j\}_{j=1}^N\), where \(N \gg M\). At each iteration \(t\), a model parameterized by \(\theta^{(t)}\) evaluates instances \(x \in \mathcal{U}\) and selects a batch \(\mathcal{B}^* \subset \mathcal{U}\) to present to an annotator oracle.

    1. Least Confidence (LC) Sampling

    The simplest uncertainty heuristic queries the instance for which the model's most confident predicted class probability is minimal:

    \[ x^*_{\text{LC}} = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \left( 1 - \max_{k \in \{1, \dots, K\}} P(y = k \mid x; \theta) \right) \]

    While computationally trivial (\(\mathcal{O}(N)\)), Least Confidence discards all information about the posterior distribution across remaining non-dominant classes.

    2. Margin Sampling

    To directly target the classifier's hyperplane boundary, Margin Sampling computes the difference between the first and second most probable class posteriors [Settles2009]:

    \[ x^*_{\text{margin}} = \arg\min_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \left( P(\hat{y}_1 \mid x; \theta) - P(\hat{y}_2 \mid x; \theta) \right) \]

    Where \(\hat{y}_1 = \arg\max_k P(y=k \mid x)\) and \(\hat{y}_2 = \arg\max_{k \ne \hat{y}_1} P(y=k \mid x)\). A small margin indicates severe ambiguity between the two leading candidates, making the point maximally informative for refining the decision boundary.

    3. Shannon Prediction Entropy Sampling

    Entropy Sampling measures the dispersion of the full categorical distribution over all \(K\) classes:

    \[ x^*_{\text{entropy}} = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \mathcal{H}(Y \mid x; \theta) = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \left( -\sum_{k=1}^K P(y = k \mid x; \theta) \log_2 P(y = k \mid x; \theta) \right) \]

    Entropy reaches its theoretical maximum \(\log_2(K)\) bits when class probabilities are completely uniform (\(P(y = k) = \frac{1}{K}\)), prioritizing points situated at multi-class intersections.

    4. Query-by-Committee (QBC) & Vote Entropy

    Rather than relying on a single model's potentially overconfident posteriors, Query-by-Committee constructs an ensemble \(\mathcal{C} = \{\theta^{(1)}, \theta^{(2)}, \dots, \theta^{(C)}\}\) (e.g., via bootstrap bagging or distinct architectures). The committee votes on each unlabeled instance, and Vote Entropy queries points with maximal disagreement:

    \[ x^*_{\text{VE}} = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \left( -\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{V(k, x)}{C} \log_2 \left( \frac{V(k, x)}{C} \right) \right) \]

    Where \(V(k, x) = \sum_{c=1}^C \mathbb{I}\left( \arg\max_j P(y = j \mid x; \theta^{(c)}) = k \right)\).

    Alternatively, the soft consensus divergence is measured via the Jensen-Shannon Divergence or average Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence from the ensemble consensus:

    \[ x^*_{\text{KL}} = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \frac{1}{C} \sum_{c=1}^C D_{\text{KL}}\left( P_{\theta^{(c)}}(\cdot \mid x) \,\|\, P_{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot \mid x) \right) \]

    Where \(P_{\mathcal{C}}(y=k \mid x) = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{c=1}^C P(y=k \mid x; \theta^{(c)})\).

    5. Core-Set & Diversity-Aware Geometric Sampling

    A fatal pathology of pure uncertainty sampling is sampling redundancy: querying 50 near-identical noisy outliers that all lie close to the decision boundary. Core-Set Active Learning [Sener2018] treats active selection as a minimax facility location problem in latent feature space \(\phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d\):

    \[ \min_{\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathcal{U}, |\mathcal{S}| \le B} \max_{i \in \mathcal{U} \setminus \mathcal{S}} \min_{j \in \mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{S}} \|\phi(x_i) - \phi(x_j)\|_2 \]

    By combining boundary uncertainty with geometric representation dispersion, hybrid acquisition functions guarantee coverage across both epistemic boundary regions and unseen data clusters.

    3. Mathematical Formalization of Weak Supervision & The Snorkel Model

    In Weak Supervision [Ratner2017, Bach2019], subject matter experts encode domain knowledge into \(m\) programmatic Labeling Functions (LFs):

    \[ \lambda_j: \mathcal{X} \to \{-1, 0, +1\}, \quad j \in \{1, \dots, m\} \]

    Where \(\lambda_j(x) = 0\) denotes abstention, and \(\{-1, +1\}\) denotes binary polarity votes. LFs can embody regex heuristics, distant supervision databases, sentiment lexicons, or zero-shot foundation model queries.

    1. Labeling Function Summary Metrics

    For a corpus of \(N\) unlabeled items, the behavior of LF \(\lambda_j\) is summarized by:

    \[ \text{Coverage: } \phi_j = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\lambda_j(x_i) \ne 0) \]
    \[ \text{Pairwise Overlap: } O_{jk} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\lambda_j(x_i) \ne 0 \land \lambda_k(x_i) \ne 0) \]
    \[ \text{Pairwise Conflict: } C_{jk} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\lambda_j(x_i) \cdot \lambda_k(x_i) = -1) \]

    2. Generative Graphical Factor Model

    Because LFs are noisy, disagree, and possess unknown accuracies, simple majority voting yields degraded pseudo-labels. Snorkel models the joint distribution over the unobserved ground truth \(Y^* \in \{-1, +1\}\) and LF outputs \(\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^m\) as a factor graph:

    \[ P_\theta(\boldsymbol{\lambda}, Y^*) = \frac{1}{Z_\theta} \exp\left( \theta_0 Y^* + \sum_{j=1}^m \theta_j \lambda_j Y^* + \sum_{j < k} \theta_{jk} \lambda_j \lambda_k \right) \]

    Where \(Z_\theta = \sum_{Y^*} \sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} \exp(\dots)\) is the partition function. Parameter \(\theta_j\) reflects the intrinsic accuracy of LF \(\lambda_j\), while \(\theta_{jk}\) captures pairwise conditional dependencies.

    3. Unsupervised Accuracy Estimation via Triplet Covariance Moments

    How can we learn parameters \(\theta\) without any ground-truth labels \(Y^*\)?

    Under the conditional independence assumption (given latent true class \(Y^*\)), the covariance between two independent labeling functions \(\lambda_j\) and \(\lambda_k\) (\(j \ne k\)) factors through their latent correlation with \(Y^*\) [Ratner2017]:

    \[ \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_k) = \mathbb{E}[\lambda_j \lambda_k] - \mathbb{E}[\lambda_j]\mathbb{E}[\lambda_k] = \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, Y^*) \cdot \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_k, Y^*) \]

    Let \(c_j = \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, Y^*)\). For any triplet of overlapping labeling functions \((j, k, l)\):

    \[ \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_k) = c_j c_k, \quad \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_l) = c_j c_l, \quad \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_k, \lambda_l) = c_k c_l \]

    Dividing the product of the first two by the third yields an exact analytical solution for \(c_j^2\):

    \[ c_j^2 = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_k) \cdot \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_l)}{\operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_k, \lambda_l)} \implies c_j = \operatorname{sgn}\left(\mathbb{E}[\lambda_j]\right) \sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_k) \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_j, \lambda_l)}{\operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_k, \lambda_l)}} \]

    Because accuracy \(\operatorname{Acc}_j = P(\lambda_j = Y^* \mid \lambda_j \ne 0) = \frac{1 + c_j / \phi_j}{2}\), the generative model estimates individual worker accuracies solely from the observable covariance matrix of their agreements and disagreements!

    4. Probabilistic Soft Pseudo-Label Aggregation

    Once LF accuracy weights \(w_j = \frac{1}{2} \ln \left( \frac{\operatorname{Acc}_j}{1 - \operatorname{Acc}_j} \right)\) are estimated, the generative model outputs a calibrated probabilistic posterior label \(\tilde{y}_i \in [0, 1]\) for downstream discriminator training:

    \[ \tilde{y}_i = P(Y_i = +1 \mid \boldsymbol{\lambda}_i) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left( -2 \sum_{j=1}^m w_j \lambda_{ij} \right)} = \sigma\left( 2 \sum_{j=1}^m w_j \lambda_{ij} \right) \]

    Downstream neural networks (e.g., BERT, ResNet) are subsequently trained by minimizing cross-entropy against soft targets \(\tilde{y}_i\), granting resilience against remaining individual LF errors.

    6. Production-Grade Python Verification Engine

    The following self-contained Python module uses Pydantic V2 to implement the core mathematical formalisms: Least Confidence, Margin Sampling, Shannon Entropy, and the analytical Triplet Covariance Moment Solver that calculates labeling function accuracies without ground truth.

    """
production_active_and_weak_supervision.py
Type-safe verification engine for Active Learning heuristics & Snorkel Generative Models.
Validated against strict Pydantic V2 schemas and automated mathematical assertions.
"""

from typing import List, Dict, Tuple, Optional
import math
import numpy as np
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator


class UnlabeledCandidate(BaseModel):
    """Pydantic V2 schema for an unlabeled instance with multi-class probabilities."""
    instance_id: str
    feature_vector: List[float]
    probabilities: List[float] = Field(..., min_length=2)

    @field_validator("probabilities")
    @classmethod
    def validate_simplex(cls, probs: List[float]) -> List[float]:
        total = sum(probs)
        if not math.isclose(total, 1.0, rel_tol=1e-3):
            raise ValueError(f"Probabilities must sum to 1.0; got {total}")
        if any(p < 0.0 or p > 1.0 for p in probs):
            raise ValueError("All probabilities must lie in [0, 1]")
        return probs


class ActiveLearningEngine:
    """Computes sample query metrics across pool of candidate instances."""

    @staticmethod
    def least_confidence(probs: List[float]) -> float:
        """Score = 1 - max_k P(y=k | x)"""
        return 1.0 - max(probs)

    @staticmethod
    def margin_sampling(probs: List[float]) -> float:
        """
        Score = -(P(top1) - P(top2)).
        Inverted so that smaller margin yields a higher acquisition score.
        """
        sorted_p = sorted(probs, reverse=True)
        return -(sorted_p[0] - sorted_p[1])

    @staticmethod
    def shannon_entropy(probs: List[float]) -> float:
        """H(p) = -sum(p * log2(p)) in bits."""
        eps = 1e-12
        return -sum(p * math.log2(max(p, eps)) for p in probs)

    @classmethod
    def rank_unlabeled_pool(
        cls, pool: List[UnlabeledCandidate], strategy: str = "entropy"
    ) -> List[Tuple[str, float]]:
        """Ranks instances in descending order of query priority."""
        metric_fn = {
            "least_confidence": cls.least_confidence,
            "margin": cls.margin_sampling,
            "entropy": cls.shannon_entropy,
        }[strategy]
        scored = [(c.instance_id, metric_fn(c.probabilities)) for c in pool]
        return sorted(scored, key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)


class LabelingFunctionMetadata(BaseModel):
    """Metadata and empirical moments for a programmatic labeling function."""
    lf_id: str
    name: str
    estimated_accuracy: float = Field(..., ge=0.5, le=1.0)
    empirical_coverage: float = Field(..., ge=0.0, le=1.0)


class SnorkelTripletSolver:
    """
    Analytically computes LF accuracies from observable pairwise covariances
    under conditional independence: Cov(Li, Lj) = c_i * c_j.
    """

    @staticmethod
    def solve_accuracies_from_covariances(
        cov_12: float, cov_13: float, cov_23: float, coverages: Tuple[float, float, float]
    ) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
        """
        Computes (acc_1, acc_2, acc_3) from empirical covariances:
        c1^2 = (cov_12 * cov_13) / cov_23
        c2^2 = (cov_12 * cov_23) / cov_13
        c3^2 = (cov_13 * cov_23) / cov_12
        acc_j = (1 + c_j / coverage_j) / 2
        """
        c1 = math.sqrt(max(1e-8, (cov_12 * cov_13) / cov_23))
        c2 = math.sqrt(max(1e-8, (cov_12 * cov_23) / cov_13))
        c3 = math.sqrt(max(1e-8, (cov_13 * cov_23) / cov_12))

        acc1 = (1.0 + c1 / coverages[0]) / 2.0
        acc2 = (1.0 + c2 / coverages[1]) / 2.0
        acc3 = (1.0 + c3 / coverages[2]) / 2.0

        return (
            min(0.999, max(0.501, acc1)),
            min(0.999, max(0.501, acc2)),
            min(0.999, max(0.501, acc3)),
        )

    @staticmethod
    def compute_soft_posterior(votes: List[int], accuracies: List[float]) -> float:
        """
        Aggregates noisy votes lambda in {-1, 0, 1} via log-odds weights:
        P(Y = +1 | lambda) = sigma(2 * sum(w_j * lambda_j))
        where w_j = 0.5 * ln(acc_j / (1 - acc_j))
        """
        log_odds_sum = 0.0
        for vote, acc in zip(votes, accuracies):
            if vote != 0:
                w_j = 0.5 * math.log(acc / (1.0 - acc))
                log_odds_sum += w_j * vote

        # Sigmoid activation
        return 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-2.0 * log_odds_sum))


def test_active_and_weak_supervision():
    """Automated test suite asserting mathematical bounds and correctness."""

    # 1. Active Learning Multi-Class Distributions
    candidates = [
        UnlabeledCandidate(
            instance_id="A_certain",
            feature_vector=[1.0, 2.0],
            probabilities=[0.90, 0.05, 0.05],
        ),
        UnlabeledCandidate(
            instance_id="B_uniform_noise",
            feature_vector=[0.0, 0.0],
            probabilities=[0.334, 0.333, 0.333],
        ),
        UnlabeledCandidate(
            instance_id="C_boundary_dilemma",
            feature_vector=[-1.0, 1.0],
            probabilities=[0.49, 0.49, 0.02],
        ),
    ]

    ranked_entropy = ActiveLearningEngine.rank_unlabeled_pool(candidates, strategy="entropy")
    ranked_margin = ActiveLearningEngine.rank_unlabeled_pool(candidates, strategy="margin")

    # Sample B has maximal entropy (near log2(3) ~ 1.585 bits)
    assert ranked_entropy[0][0] == "B_uniform_noise"
    assert math.isclose(ranked_entropy[0][1], math.log2(3.0), rel_tol=1e-2)

    # Sample C has minimal margin difference (0.49 - 0.49 = 0.00 -> top rank in margin)
    assert ranked_margin[0][0] == "C_boundary_dilemma"
    assert math.isclose(ranked_margin[0][1], 0.0, abs_tol=1e-4)

    # Sample A has lowest priority in all acquisition metrics
    assert ranked_entropy[-1][0] == "A_certain"
    assert ranked_margin[-1][0] == "A_certain"

    # 2. Snorkel Triplet Covariance Moment Solver
    # Ground truth: 3 LFs with 100% coverage, true accuracies [0.90, 0.80, 0.70]
    # Latent correlations c_j = 2*acc_j - 1 -> c1 = 0.8, c2 = 0.6, c3 = 0.4
    # Theoretical covariances:
    cov_12 = 0.8 * 0.6  # 0.48
    cov_13 = 0.8 * 0.4  # 0.32
    cov_23 = 0.6 * 0.4  # 0.24

    solved_accs = SnorkelTripletSolver.solve_accuracies_from_covariances(
        cov_12, cov_13, cov_23, coverages=(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)
    )

    assert math.isclose(solved_accs[0], 0.90, rel_tol=1e-4)
    assert math.isclose(solved_accs[1], 0.80, rel_tol=1e-4)
    assert math.isclose(solved_accs[2], 0.70, rel_tol=1e-4)

    # 3. Soft Posterior Calibration
    # 3 LFs with accuracies [0.90, 0.80, 0.55]
    # Case: LF1 (+1), LF2 (+1), LF3 (-1) -> LF3 is a noisy rule with low accuracy
    accs = [0.90, 0.80, 0.55]
    votes_conflict = [1, 1, -1]
    posterior = SnorkelTripletSolver.compute_soft_posterior(votes_conflict, accs)

    # High-accuracy LF1 and LF2 should comfortably override low-accuracy LF3
    assert posterior > 0.90, f"Expected posterior > 0.90, got {posterior}"

    # Abstention test: [0, 0, 0] should produce 0.50 prior
    prior = SnorkelTripletSolver.compute_soft_posterior([0, 0, 0], accs)
    assert math.isclose(prior, 0.50, abs_tol=1e-5)

    print("=== Production Active Learning & Weak Supervision Verification ===")
    print(f"Entropy Ranking: {ranked_entropy}")
    print(f"Margin Ranking: {ranked_margin}")
    print(f"Analytically Recovered LF Accuracies: {[round(a, 3) for a in solved_accs]}")
    print(f"Resolved Conflict Soft Posterior P(Y=1): {posterior:.4f}")
    print("✓ All Pydantic V2 schemas and mathematical bounds validated successfully!")

    7. Key Engineering Takeaways

    1. Information Density Maximization: Active learning queries instances directly at the classification boundary (low margin, maximal entropy), yielding equivalent test set generalization with \(40\% \text{ to } 70\%\) fewer labeled instances than passive uniform sampling.
    2. Unsupervised Accuracy Discovery: Snorkel's generative model resolves conflicts and learns worker/LF accuracies without inspecting ground-truth labels by observing empirical covariance moments under conditional independence.
    3. Core-Set Geometric Safeguards: Pure uncertainty querying suffers from batch sampling redundancy; production pipelines must incorporate latent core-set diversity constraints to avoid clustering queries around identical noisy outliers.
    4. Soft Labels Outperform Hard Thresholds: Training downstream deep discriminators on probabilistic soft posteriors \(\tilde{y}_i \in [0, 1]\) prevents overconfidence and provides statistical regularization against remaining heuristic errors.

    8. Scenario-Based Diagnostic Micro-Checks

    Micro-Check 1: Bimodal Dilemmas — Margin vs. Shannon Entropy Sampling

    In a 10-class diagnostic classification task, Instance A outputs predicted probabilities \([0.49, 0.49, 0.002, \dots]\) (a binary impasse between two diagnoses). Instance B outputs \([0.15, 0.15, 0.14, 0.14, 0.12, \dots]\) (diffuse uncertainty across 8 classes). Which acquisition strategy will prioritize Instance A, and which will prioritize Instance B?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Margin Sampling prioritizes Instance A; Shannon Entropy prioritizes Instance B.

    1. Margin Sampling focuses strictly on the top-2 classes: $\(\Delta_{\text{margin}}(A) = 0.49 - 0.49 = 0.00\)$ $\(\Delta_{\text{margin}}(B) = 0.15 - 0.15 = 0.00\)$ If Instance A were \([0.491, 0.490]\) and Instance B were \([0.16, 0.15]\), Margin Sampling directly detects the critical decision boundary impasse of Instance A.
    2. Shannon Entropy measures the total dispersion across all classes: $\(\mathcal{H}(A) \approx -2 \cdot (0.49 \log_2 0.49) \approx 1.01\text{ bits}\)$ $\(\mathcal{H}(B) \approx -6 \cdot (0.15 \log_2 0.15) \approx 2.95\text{ bits}\)$ Entropy sampling prefers Instance B because the model knows virtually nothing about any class. However, for refining decision boundaries, Margin Sampling is often preferred in high-cardinality multi-class tasks because diffuse uncertainty often indicates out-of-distribution junk.
    Micro-Check 2: The Cold-Start & Sampling Bias Trap in Active Learning

    Why does Active Learning catastrophically fail if deployed on an untrained deep neural network at Epoch 0 with randomly initialized weights, and how does Warm-Start Seeding prevent this pathology?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Random weights produce uncalibrated, geometrically meaningless uncertainty, causing severe sampling bias.

    Active acquisition functions assume that posterior probabilities \(P(y \mid x; \theta)\) correspond to actual geometric distance to the optimal decision boundary. With randomly initialized weights \(\theta_0\): $\(P(y=k \mid x; \theta_0) \approx \text{Uniform Noise}\)$ Querying maximal entropy or minimal margin selects arbitrary artifacts of initialization rather than true boundary instances. Furthermore, once an active learner samples an unrepresentative biased batch, subsequent retraining overfits to this bias, causing the model to never explore other regions of the feature space (the active learning confirmation trap).

    Engineering Solution: Always execute a Warm-Start Pilot Cohort (e.g., \(N=500\) to \(2,000\) uniformly random samples) to establish calibrated representation manifolds and initial hyperplanes before launching active sampling loops.

    Micro-Check 3: Correlated Labeling Functions in Weak Supervision

    Suppose two labeling functions \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) are identical duplicates (e.g., two regexes searching for synonyms of the same word). What happens if they are combined with a third independent labeling function \(\lambda_3\) using naive majority voting, and how does Snorkel handle this dependency?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    Naive majority voting double-counts correlated rules, allowing redundant heuristics to overrule more accurate independent evidence.

    If \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) are identical and output \(-1\), their collective weight in majority voting is \(2\). Even if an expert independent rule \(\lambda_3\) with \(95\%\) accuracy votes \(+1\), majority voting outputs \(-1\) (\(2 \text{ vs } 1\)), compounding the correlation error.

    In Snorkel's generative factor model, correlated LFs exhibit high non-zero conditional covariance: $\(\operatorname{Cov}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \mid Y^*) \gg 0\)$ By estimating pairwise interaction parameters \(\theta_{jk} \lambda_j \lambda_k\) in the factor graph, the generative model penalizes overlapping mutual information, effectively shrinking the combined weight of \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) back to that of a single independent voter.

    Micro-Check 4: Anchoring Bias in Model-Assisted Pre-Labeling

    In autonomous vehicle bounding-box annotation, human annotators reviewing model pre-labeled bounding boxes are \(4\times\) faster than human annotators drawing boxes from scratch. However, downstream model recall on small pedestrians drops by \(8\%\). What cognitive pathology causes this, and how can the QA pipeline detect it?

    Reveal Detailed Solution

    This is caused by Human Anchoring Bias and Asymmetric Verification Effort.

    When presented with an existing bounding box, annotators subconsciously accept the candidate as valid (confirmation bias), adjusting only the coordinates slightly. Crucially, verifying false positives is cognitively easy, but identifying false negatives (missed objects) requires full-canvas visual scanning. Because annotators are incentivized for speed, they overlook small, faint, or occluded pedestrians that the base model failed to propose.

    Engineering Countermeasures: 1. Honeypot Injection: Insert synthetically stripped candidates (known ground-truth pedestrians where pre-labels are intentionally omitted) into the review queue to measure annotator discovery recall. 2. Dual-Blind Redundancy on Edge Regions: Unprelabeled raw frames for complex urban intersections with high pedestrian density. 3. Confidence-Gated Display: Only display pre-labels when model confidence exceeds \(\tau_{\text{high}} = 0.85\); present raw imagery when confidence is moderate (\(0.30 \le p \le 0.85\)) to force active visual inspection.
